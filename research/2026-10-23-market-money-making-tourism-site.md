# Research Report — Money-Making Tourism Website

> **Type:** market (+ competitive, trend) | **Date:** 2026-10-23 | **Constraint:** the target market was left open — this report compares Thai-domestic vs inbound-to-Thailand vs global/English and recommends one.
>
> **Goals:**
> - Find data-grounded ideas for a tourism website whose primary purpose is to make money.
> - Decide which of the three markets is most worth building for.
> - Identify the most viable monetization models and realistic earnings.
> - Surface downside/threats (AI-search disruption, Google updates, platform risk) for an honest go/no-go.

>

## Executive Summary

A money-making tourism website is viable in 2026 — but **only in a narrow, non-obvious form**, and the default form most people picture (a destination travel blog monetized by display ads and affiliate links off Google search traffic) is one of the worst things a solo builder could start this year. Every one of the seven research axes converged on the same verdict independently: AI Overviews, rising zero-click search, and Google's Helpful Content Update have structurally gutted informational travel content, and almost none of the lost traffic is being recaptured through AI citations (ChatGPT accounts for ~0.02% of publisher referral traffic, per Chartbeat/Reuters Institute data in Press Gazette, accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established).

**The market pick is inbound-to-Thailand / SEA, in English, aimed at Western, Indian, digital-nomad, and premium-experience travelers — not Thai-domestic, and not generic global/English.** The decisive fact is a roughly tenfold geographic gap in monetization: Western-reader display RPMs run ~$15–35 for travel while Thai-audience rates are about a tenth of that (YouTube CPM Thailand ~$1.30 vs US ~$14.67), and the Thai-domestic audience, though six times larger in trip volume, spends and monetizes a fraction per visitor (all RPM figures Low-tier vendor estimates, but the ~10x direction is consistent across every source). Generic global/English is the biggest, highest-RPM audience but also the most saturated, most AI-exposed, and most consolidated niche on the web — a solo builder loses there head-on. The sweet spot captures Western/Indian RPMs while exploiting Thailand/SEA depth that the global incumbents ignore.

**The top recommended project is a Thailand/SEA digital-nomad and visa-logistics hub built as a directory-plus-tools-plus-service, not a blog** — visa-rules-by-nationality lookups, DTV (Destination Thailand Visa) guidance, cost-of-living and nomad-base data, and a coworking/long-stay accommodation database, monetized through an attached paid service (visa/relocation help), travel-insurance affiliate, eSIM, long-stay accommodation affiliate, and an owned email list. This shape is favored because it answers transactional/lookup queries that AI still routes clicks to (transactional intent is only ~2–3% of AI-cited query volume, versus informational at ~71–92%), it matches the builder's existing Supabase/directory skillset, and no SEA-specific nomad/vertical directory with published metrics surfaced in the research — a genuine whitespace signal.

**The single biggest risk is time-to-traffic, not build cost or even AI disruption directly.** Infrastructure is trivially cheap ($0–50/month). The binding constraint is the many months to well over a year of consistent, differentiated content a brand-new domain needs to reach the ~25,000 monthly pageviews that unlock a premium ad network — and no reliable benchmark for that timeline exists (a real gap). Compounding risks: directories cold-start hard on data acquisition and plateau without freshness plus an attached higher-value service (Nomad List is the usual cautionary case, though its revenue figures are unverified and conflicting), and direct affiliate terms can be withdrawn unilaterally (Booking.com's May 2025 "Bookinggeddon" ended direct deals for thousands of small partners on 30 days' notice and moved them to networks, per Skift). Mediavine Journey now opens at 1,000 Tier-1 sessions/month (since 2026-01-15), so premium display can start earlier than older "50K sessions" figures suggest.

**Bottom line (honest go/no-go):** GO, but only on a tightly-scoped English-language inbound/SEA tool-or-directory with an owned-audience and service layer designed in from day one. NO-GO on a generic Thailand travel blog, and NO-GO on a Thai-language domestic site as a money-making bet. The money that survives in 2026 is owned-audience and service revenue, not ad/affiliate RPM; treat search and social as top-of-funnel feeding an audience you own, not as the monetization pipe itself.

## Monetization Models — How Tourism Websites Make Money

> No official affiliate-program pages were reachable; all commission rates come from affiliate networks, directories, and blogs (Medium-to-Low confidence) and must be verified on each program's live terms before modeling revenue. Full detail and conflicts in `monetization-models.md`.

**The revenue menu.** A solo-operated travel site realistically draws on six or seven streams, and almost every documented site blends several rather than relying on one: display advertising, travel affiliate programs (OTA, tour, hotel, car-rental, insurance), sponsored posts and brand deals, info products (guides, ebooks, courses), paid services (trip planning, itineraries), memberships/communities, and — for a different breed of site — directory listing fees and lead generation. Display ads and affiliate are the two load-bearing pillars; sponsorships are a minor topping for most. One documented mid-sized blog that logged ~$6,821 in a single late-2025 month split it as display ads 54%, affiliate 29%, "other" 15.5%, and sponsored content just 1.5%, according to a secondary guide citing the Living the Dream RTW income report ([lovable.dev](https://lovable.dev/guides/how-to-make-money-as-travel-blogger) citing [Living the Dream RTW](https://www.livingthedreamrtw.com/income-and-traffic-report), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low).

**Affiliate commission rates (2024–2026).** Affiliate is the most-documented but also the most-conflicting data point, because published rates come from networks rather than the programs themselves and because the headline percentage often describes a different base than readers assume. The single most important structural fact, stressed by the track360 operator benchmark, is that Booking.com and Expedia pay a percentage of the *OTA's own commission*, not of the trip price — so a "25–40% share" of Booking's ~10–15% commission nets the publisher only a low-single-digit percentage of booking value, which is why other tables simply list Booking.com as "~4%" of booking value with a one-session cookie ([track360](https://track360.io/blog/best-travel-affiliate-programs-2026-operator-rate-card-benchmark), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low; [Travelpayouts](https://www.travelpayouts.com/blog/best-affiliate-programs-for-travel-sites/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low). The consolidated current ranges, all directional and verification-pending:

- **Booking.com (hotels):** ~4% of booking value (≈25–40% of Booking's own commission); one-session cookie; paid on completed stay (track360; Travelpayouts) — Medium.
- **Agoda (hotels, SEA-founded):** tiered ~4–7% of booking value on completed stays, scaling with monthly volume; very short cookie (~1 day per some sources); reported ~$200 payout threshold ([ecomobi](https://ecomobi.com/agoda-affiliate-program-earn-4-8-commission-with-ecomobi/), [track360 Agoda teardown](https://track360.io/blog/agoda-affiliate-program-operator-teardown-2026), [getlasso](https://getlasso.co/affiliate/agoda/), [UpPromote](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-directory/agoda/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low) — the 4–7% tiered shape is the consensus.
- **Expedia (EAN / TAAP):** ~2–7% on Expedia's commission; cookie reported as both ~7 days and 30 days (**conflict**) — Medium.
- **GetYourGuide (tours & activities):** ~8% base with volume lifts; 30–31 day cookie; multiple sources agree — **High**.
- **Viator (tours & activities):** 8–10%; 30-day cookie (**conflict:** track360/UpPromote ~8%, Travelpayouts 10%) — Medium.
- **Klook (tours & activities, Asia focus):** ~2–6.5% by category; niche products like eSIM up to ~20%; ~30-day cookie ([Travelpayouts](https://www.travelpayouts.com/blog/best-affiliate-programs-for-travel-sites/), [Involve Asia](https://app.involve.asia/directory/klook-affiliate-program), [ecomobi](https://ecomobi.com/klook-affiliate-program-review), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low).
- **Highest-margin categories:** travel insurance ~10–40% of premium (often the single best travel category); DiscoverCars 23–54% of advertiser revenue with a 365-day cookie; Hostelworld 40% of the deposit; AirHelp 15–29% (track360; Travelpayouts) — Medium.
- **Travelpayouts (aggregator):** no join requirement; passes through brand-set rates (~1–10%+), reportedly paying partners ~50–70% rev share; 30–90 day cookies vary by brand — Medium.

The analytically sound warning from track360 is to compare programs on *effective payout per confirmed booking*, not headline percentage, because a high rate on a thin OTA commission base can pay less than a lower CPA on full booking value.

**The critical structural risk — Booking.com's 2025 affiliate purge ("Bookinggeddon").** According to Skift, in late May 2025 Booking.com abruptly ended affiliate partnerships with large numbers of travel bloggers, giving 30 days' notice and no official explanation; Travel Massive estimated "thousands" were dropped, and displaced affiliates were pointed toward third-party networks (CJ, Awin, Affnetmedia) ([Skift](https://skift.com/2025/05/30/why-booking-com-cut-thousands-of-affiliate-partners-and-what-comes-next/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). *This suggests* a concrete, demonstrated platform risk: direct-OTA affiliate income can be switched off overnight on a volume judgment the solo builder does not control — which argues for accessing OTAs through an aggregator (Travelpayouts) and diversifying across programs rather than depending on any single direct relationship.

**Display RPMs — travel is a mid-to-low niche.** A February 2026 niche study (47 independent blogs, anonymized GA + revenue) put travel display RPM at $10–18 and travel *affiliate* RPM far higher at $30–70, versus personal finance at $25–45 display ([earnifyhub](https://earnifyhub.com/blog/blog-traffic-vs-revenue-reality-check.php), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low — small self-reported sample). Other low-tier sources converge: travel earns roughly $21/1,000 visitors on Mediavine and ~$24 on Raptive, and display RPM scales heavily with US traffic share (~$18 at 25% US vs ~$33–35 at 50%+ US) ([MotionInvest](https://www.motioninvest.com/), [Stay22 community](https://community.stay22.com/best-ad-networks-for-monetizing-travel-blogs), [This Week in Blogging](https://thisweekinblogging.com/mediavine-raptive-requirements/) via lovable.dev, accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). Mediavine itself declines to publish niche benchmarks and calculates session RPM rather than per-pageview ([Mediavine](https://www.mediavine.com/mediavine-rpm), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). *The defensible working figure* is ~$12–25 session RPM for travel, trending low for non-US audiences and toward $30+ only for heavily US/Tier-1 traffic.

**The revenue ladder.** No source provides an authoritative earnings-by-traffic table for travel; the table below combines the sourced RPM ranges with representative per-site examples (sourced) and the agent's traffic→$ arithmetic (flagged). The earnifyhub study's own documented high end anchors it: one ~85K-session travel site earned ~$12,400/month from affiliate + digital guides (an effective ~$145 RPM), far above what display alone yields at that traffic.

| Monthly visits | Realistic revenue (sourced RPMs × agent arithmetic) | Best model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ~10K | ~$100–250/mo | affiliate + info products (display negligible; below Mediavine floor) |
| ~50K | ~$1,500–5,000/mo | affiliate-led; display unlocks via Mediavine |
| ~200K | $5,000–15,000+/mo | affiliate/products out-earn; display a predictable base layer |

Across all levels the documented pattern is: below ~10K visits prioritize affiliate and digital products; at 10K–50K layer in display via Mediavine Journey; sponsored content becomes viable with ~10K+ engaged social followers ([lovable.dev](https://lovable.dev/guides/how-to-make-money-as-travel-blogger), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). The sobering context: a ProBlogger survey found **63% of monetizing bloggers earn under ~$1,300/year**, and Productive Blogging data puts even years 3–5 at $500–$9,000/month — the median outcome is low and the distribution extremely long-tailed ([Productive Blogging](https://www.productiveblogging.com/how-much-do-bloggers-earn/) and [ProBlogger](https://problogger.com/) via lovable.dev, accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low).

**Which model wins, and resilience — the most decision-relevant finding.** At low traffic, affiliate and digital products win decisively because their effective RPM dwarfs display's and premium ad networks are simply closed to small sites; travel's high transaction values mean one booked tour or insurance policy can outweigh thousands of impressions. At high traffic, display finally becomes a meaningful base layer, but affiliate and products still out-earn it per visitor — so the best strategy is a stack, not a pivot. The deeper point is resilience: both display and affiliate monetize Google search visitors who are increasingly intercepted before they reach the site, and travel is among the hardest-hit verticals. The Planet D shut down after AI Overviews (NPR reports a ~90% decline; UCLA Anderson gives ~50% — the two agree on direction and catastrophic scale, [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/07/31/nx-s1-5484118/google-ai-overview-online-publishers), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established; [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/large-language-models-are-pushing-the-web-toward-zero-clicks/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established). Similarweb (via Barron's) found travel search referrals down ~20% YoY and BrightEdge put AI-Overview saturation on travel SERPs at 38% ([via WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/how-googles-ai-overviews-are-destroying-traffic-for-media-publishers-and-content-creators/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low aggregation of Established data). *The synthesis conclusion:* the most resilient streams are owned-audience plays — email lists, memberships/communities, info products, and services (trip planning documented at $300–$580+) — not ad/affiliate RPM. A durable 2026 travel site should treat search as top-of-funnel feeding an owned audience, not as a pipe to monetize directly.

## Market Opportunity

### Market A — Thailand (domestic travelers + inbound tourists)

Thailand is one of the largest and most mature tourism economies in Asia, and the key strategic fact for a bootstrapped builder is that its two audiences behave almost oppositely: a large, high-value, English-and-Chinese-speaking inbound market that monetizes well but is volatile and competitive, and a very large, resilient Thai-domestic market that monetizes poorly in ad terms.

**(a) Inbound size, recovery, source countries.** Thailand's inbound recovery peaked in 2024 and then *reversed* in 2025 — a counterintuitive fact for anyone sizing the market today. After roughly 27–28 million arrivals in 2023, arrivals rebounded to about 35.3–35.5 million in 2024, generating ~1.66–1.67 trillion baht in foreign-visitor revenue, per Ministry of Tourism and Sports figures restated by Minister Sorawong Thienthong ([Bangkok Post](https://www.bangkokpost.com/thailand/general/2931152/kingdom-of-thailand-welcomed-35m-in-2024), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established; [Sakshi Post / IANS](https://www.sakshipost.com/news/thailand-receives-over-355-million-foreign-tourists-2024-365929), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). Then 2025 declined to **32,974,321 arrivals (−7.23%)**, with foreign-tourist revenue of **~1.53 trillion baht (~$49 billion, −4.71%)** — the first non-pandemic annual decline in a decade, which Skift attributes to safety concerns, natural disasters, political uncertainty, and a stronger baht ([Khaosod English](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/tourism/2025/12/11/thailand-tourist-arrivals-fall-to-32m-amid-challenging-2025/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established; [Skift](https://skift.com/2026/01/06/why-thailands-foreign-tourist-numbers-slipped-in-2025-and-what-it-means-for-2026/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established). TAT's 2026 goal is 36.7 million ([Pattaya Mail](https://www.pattayamail.com/thailandnews/new-year-tourism-boom-pushes-thailand-past-2025-target-tat-bets-on-lisa-effect-for-2026-532034), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established) — a return to roughly the 2024 peak, not new growth. On economic weight, tourism contributed ~14.16% of GDP in Q2 2024 on a broad (direct plus indirect) basis ([Bangkok Post](https://www.bangkokpost.com/business/2610502/tat-upbeat-on-obtaining-full-budget-in-2024), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). *This suggests* a very large but flat-to-recovering market where demand swings hard on safety news.

**Source-country mix** decides which language/audience to target. According to Ministry figures, 2024's top markets were **China 6.70M, Malaysia 4.93M, India 2.12M, South Korea 1.86M, Russia 1.72M** ([Bangkok Post](https://www.bangkokpost.com/thailand/general/2931152/kingdom-of-thailand-welcomed-35m-in-2024), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established). By 2025 the order flipped at the top: **Malaysia 4.52M (−8.71%) overtook China 4.47M (−33.55%)**, with **India rising to #3 at ~2.48M (+16–18%)** — the one strong-growth segment ([Khaosod English](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/tourism/2025/12/11/thailand-tourist-arrivals-fall-to-32m-amid-challenging-2025/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established; [Skift — India](https://skift.com/2026/01/07/how-india-traveled-in-2025-what-the-data-shows/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established). *The implication:* the biggest *controllable* English-adjacent opportunities are Indian and Malaysian travelers (both English-literate); the Chinese collapse is the dominant risk.

**(b) Domestic market.** The domestic market is large and, unlike inbound, grew through 2025 — the market's shock absorber. In 2025 Thai travelers took about **202.37M domestic trips (+2.70%)**, generating roughly **1.17 trillion baht (+3.69%)**, so total tourism revenue was ~2.70 trillion baht, down just 1.26% overall as domestic resilience offset the inbound drop ([Asia News Network / The Nation](https://asianews.network/thailand-tourism-slips-in-2025-despite-domestic-growth/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; [Pattaya Mail](https://www.pattayamail.com/news/thailands-tourism-sees-revenue-dip-as-domestic-travel-shows-resilience-532748), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). But scale is not value: domestic trips outnumber foreign arrivals roughly 6-to-1, yet spend only ~5,800 baht/trip versus ~47,000 baht per foreign arrival (the agent's calculation from the figures above). *The takeaway:* the Thai-language domestic audience is huge in traffic terms but generates a fraction of the ad/affiliate revenue per visitor.

**(c) OTA penetration.** Thailand sits inside a fast-growing online-travel market: the e-Conomy SEA 2024 report (Google/Temasek/Bain) put Thailand's online-travel GMV at **~$10 billion in 2024 (+32%), projected to ~$20 billion by 2030** ([Bangkok Post — e-Conomy SEA 2024](https://www.bangkokpost.com/business/general/2897723/digital-economy-seen-growing-19-in-2024), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; [Google Thailand blog](https://thailand.googleblog.com/2024/11/eConomy-SEA-2024-report.html), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). Online travel is the single largest-growing Thai digital vertical — a strong signal of monetizable booking intent. **Agoda dominates Thailand** (effectively its home market): a Q1 2024 YouGov study found Agoda led brand awareness at 56% and purchase-consideration at 51%, ahead of Traveloka and Booking.com ([YouGov](https://business.yougov.com/content/49686-thailand-online-travel-agencies-best-at-turning-awareness-to-purchase-intent-q1-2024), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established), while Klook dominates activities/tours and Trip.com captures Chinese bookings ([Cloudbeds](https://www.cloudbeds.com/articles/analysis-of-major-online-travel-agencies-otas/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). No audited country-level OTA booking-share data was found, so the Agoda-dominance claim rests on awareness/consideration plus channel rankings — Medium confidence.

**(d) Digital behavior.** The inbound audience splits sharply by nationality, and this is the most decision-relevant behavioral finding. Chinese travelers live in a walled ecosystem — Xiaohongshu (RED) and Douyin to research, Ctrip/Trip.com to book — largely unreachable by an independent English or Thai site; a China Trading Desk survey of 15,000 found ~40% plan via social, with Xiaohongshu preferred among 18–29s ([Phocuswire](https://www.phocuswire.com/China-trading-desk-report-GenZ-women-international-travel), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). Western/English-speaking and Indian travelers split Google plus social: short-form video (TikTok/Instagram) wins the *inspiration* stage, while Google and long-form content hold up better for *logistics* (visas, itineraries, "best area to stay," island-hopping routes) ([FTN News](https://ftnnews.com/?p=78382), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low; [Travel Daily News / MMGY](https://www.traveldailynews.com/post/mmgy-global-provides-expert-insights-on-booking-holidays-through-social-media), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). That planning/logistics layer is the window an SEO-driven guide site can still occupy. *However,* no authoritative 2024–2025 survey measuring how inbound tourists specifically use English travel blogs/guides was found — the claim that "blogs retain booking-stage value" is **inferred, Low confidence**.

**(e) Niches.** Several concrete gaps stand out. First, the **Destination Thailand Visa (DTV)** for digital nomads, launched mid-2024 (5-year validity, up to 180 days per stay), reportedly crossed ~35,000 applicants in its first year — visa/long-stay/nomad logistics are high-intent, evergreen, English-language, and poorly served ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/tourism/thailand-sees-spike-in-digital-nomad-visas-uae-remote-workers-take-notice-1.500221056), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Established — single unofficial media figure). Second, the **Indian market** is the standout growth segment (~2.48M arrivals in 2025, +16–18%, English-literate, underserved versus saturated generic "Thailand travel"), driven by new direct routes into secondary cities ([Skift — India](https://skift.com/2026/01/07/how-india-traveled-in-2025-what-the-data-shows/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established; [Pattaya Mail](https://www.pattayamail.com/news/indian-tourist-boom-in-pattaya-expected-as-flights-and-growth-surge-509542), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). Third, TAT is **pivoting toward higher-spending travelers** after the Chinese decline, favoring premium/experience niches ([Jing Daily](https://jingdaily.com/intels/2025-01/07/chinese-tourists-lead-thailand-s-tourism-recovery), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Established). The 2025 Chinese collapse (−33.55%) was triggered by the January 2025 Wang Xing kidnapping and scam-compound fears ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/chinese-travelers-cancel-lunar-new-year-trips-thailand-safety-concerns-rcna189750), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; [Bangkok Post — scam fears](https://www.bangkokpost.com/thailand/general/2950162/scam-centre-fears-keep-chinese-tourists-on-edge-in-thailand), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established); any rebound would flow through Chinese platforms, not an English site.

**(f) Monetization — the decisive finding (~10x geographic RPM gap).** This is where the two audiences diverge most. Agoda's affiliate program (the natural APAC fit) pays ~4–7% of completed-stay value, tiered, with a short (~1-day) cookie — figures from affiliate directories, not Agoda's own terms, so **Low confidence; verify on Agoda's partner page** ([Track360 — Agoda teardown](https://track360.io/blog/agoda-affiliate-program-operator-teardown-2026), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low; [getlasso](https://getlasso.co/affiliate/agoda/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). Display ads track where the *reader* is, not the content's topic: Western-inbound readers earn Mediavine travel RPM ~$15–35 ([Stay22 community](https://community.stay22.com/best-ad-networks-for-monetizing-travel-blogs), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low), but Thai-audience rates are roughly a tenth — YouTube CPM Thailand ~**$1.30** versus US ~**$14.67**, with modeled Thai display RPMs under $1 ([AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/translating-videos-into-thai-data-tools-and-cultural-nuance), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low; [creaticalc](https://creaticalc.com/blog/youtube-cpm-rates-by-country), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low; [Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/data/youtube-adsense-rpm/th), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). The exact numbers are soft (all Low-tier), but the ~10x gap direction is consistent across every source. **The conclusion the data supports:** a solo money-making site should target English-language inbound tourists (Western + Indian + nomad + premium), not Thai-domestic or Chinese markets — domestic is bigger in traffic but far lower in revenue/visitor, and Chinese traffic is unreachable via an independent site.

### Market B — Global / English travel content & affiliate

The ceiling is enormous but the slice a solo content site can touch is a thin layer far down the stack.

**(a) Market size.** The WTTC projected travel/tourism to contribute a record ~$11.1 trillion to global GDP in 2024 (~10% of world GDP), growing to ~$16T by 2034 ([WTTC via Gulf Today](https://gulftoday.ae/Business/2024/09/03/Travel-industry-to-contribute-record-$11-trillion-to-global-GDP-in-2024), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Primary; [WTTC via TravelPulse](https://www.travelpulse.com/news/impacting-travel/travel-industry-to-set-new-record-for-global-gdp-contribution), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established) — a whole-economy number that only matters as the top of the funnel. One level down, Phocuswright (the most credible travel analyst) put global travel **gross bookings at ~$1.6T in 2024, on track to ~$1.8T by 2027**, of which **online bookings were ~$1T, growing ~9%/yr** toward ~$1.2T by 2026 ([Phocuswright via altexsoft](https://www.altexsoft.com/travel-industry-news/gross-bookings-rebound-to-1-6t-digital-bookings-take-over/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established; [Phocuswright via TravelPulse](https://www.travelpulse.com/news/features/online-travel-bookings-to-reach-1-2-trillion-by-end-of-2026), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established). Commercial research-firm "online travel market" figures vary roughly 3x by definition — OTA-only ~$94B (Skift Research) vs broad $560–670B (IMARC/GMI/Statista/ResearchAndMarkets) vs ~$1T total online bookings (Phocuswright) — and the broad ones are mostly undisclosed-methodology SEO-bait, so **do not quote a single point estimate** ([Skift Research](https://research.skift.com/reports/global-online-travel-sector-market-estimates-2025/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; [IMARC](https://imarcgroup.com/online-travel-market), [Statista](https://statista.com/topics/2704/online-travel-market), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low–Medium, tier: Low–Established). **No credible travel-affiliate-specific market size exists** — only proxies (the broad affiliate industry ~$15.7–18.5B global in 2024, all Low-tier/conflicting, [Wix](https://www.wix.com/blog/affiliate-marketing-statistics), [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/affiliate-marketing-statistics/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). *Any precise "travel affiliate TAM" claim is effectively fabricated.*

**(b) The blog business model — contracting.** The weight of evidence is that the independent travel-blog model is contracting, squeezed from both the traffic side (Google + AI) and the monetization side. Google's August 2022 Helpful Content Update plus the March 2024 core update hit travel publishers hard: one study of 671 travel publishers found 78% lost organic traffic, some by 90%+ ([Digitaloft](https://digitaloft.co.uk/insights/the-impact-of-googles-helpful-content-update-hcu-on-travel-publishers/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low — single-author study); late-2024 updates partially and unevenly reversed some losses ([via Travelpayouts](https://www.travelpayouts.com/blog/google-updates-2024/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). Monetization is thin: Travelpayouts (a vendor) states only ~4% of a travel blog's pages generate any revenue, and display revenue is falling faster than affiliate ([Travelpayouts](https://special.travelpayouts.com/travel-industry), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). Underlying affiliate economics are modest: tours pay best (Viator ~8%/30-day; GetYourGuide ~8–10%), hotels ~3–7%, car rental ~4–8%, flights ~1–3%, Booking.com in-session only ([Affilimate](https://affilimate.io/blog/travel-affiliate-programs/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low). *The data points are almost all vendor/self-reported but converge consistently enough to indicate real pressure, with affiliate now more resilient than display for survivors.*

**(c) Saturation — very high.** English travel content is among the most SEO-competitive niches; head terms ("best hotels in [city]") are effectively closed to new domains, owned by Booking/Expedia/Tripadvisor plus big media, so guides uniformly tell new sites to win low-difficulty long-tail queries first (KD<30, hand-checking page-one competition) ([Ahrefs blog](https://ahrefs.com/blog/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established on method; [Stay22 community](https://community.stay22.com/techniques-for-travel-blogs-to-excel-in-search-results), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). *Flagged:* Stay22's "saturation forecast" pages carry precise-looking but uncited and internally contradictory stats ("15 million creators / 0.7% success" on one page vs "25 million creators / 98% failure" on another) — these are **not quotable** ([Stay22 saturation pages](https://community.stay22.com/market-saturation-for-travel-bloggers-in-2026), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low).

**(d) Audience/demand.** English accounts for ~49–52% of all web content, far ahead of second-place Spanish (~6%) — the largest, highest-RPM reachable audience ([Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/262946/share-of-the-most-common-languages-on-the-internet/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established; [Oberlo](https://oberlo.com/statistics/most-used-languages-on-the-internet), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established). But that reach is exactly why it is the most contested — the large audience and the saturation in (c) are two sides of the same coin. *Gaps:* this measures content share, not the share of *users* who read English; and **no reliable global travel-search-volume figure** was found (only conflicting India-only numbers).

**(e) Discovery shift — adverse for text SEO.** Discovery is moving off the ten-blue-links page. A Forbes Advisor/Talker survey found 45% of Gen Z prefer social-searching on TikTok/Instagram over Google and 57% overall use YouTube to search ([via Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4121680.html), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/isn-t-clear-go-to-platform-social-search-among-gen-z), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). On AI answers: travel-site search referrals fell ~20% after AI Overviews, zero-click searches rose 56%→69% after AIO launched, and ~68% of US searches were click-free in early 2026 ([Search Engine Roundtable / Similarweb](https://www.seroundtable.com/similarweb-google-zero-click-search-growth-39706.html), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; [UCLA Anderson Review / SparkToro](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/large-language-models-are-pushing-the-web-toward-zero-clicks/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). *The pure-text affiliate blog is the most-exposed format;* one partial counterweight is that transactional/booking queries still require a click (Low confidence).

**(f) Benchmarks — even flagships shrinking.** Per SimilarWeb (order-of-magnitude), The Points Guy drew ~9.4M visits in March 2025 (**−21% YoY**) and Nomadic Matt ~567K (**−48% YoY**) ([View From The Wing](https://viewfromthewing.com/march-2025-frequent-flyer-site-rankings-frequent-miler-jumps-36-the-points-guy-falls-21-doctor-of-credit-holds-steady/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). A new blog typically earns ~$0–120/month in year one, with meaningful income taking 1–5 years and most never reaching it ([TravelFeed](https://travelfeed.com/blog/is-travel-blogging-worth-it), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). *Against a ~$1T online-booking pool, a successful small site captures five-figure annual revenue — functionally 0% market share;* the game is ranking long-tail clusters, not capturing share. Adjacent, travel media is consolidating — Red Ventures owns The Points Guy and Lonely Planet ([Skift](https://skift.com/2020/12/01/the-points-guy-owner-red-ventures-buys-lonely-planet-in-travel-guidebook-play/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established), while Skift laid off staff in 2024 and Tripadvisor cut ~150 in 2025 ([talkingbiznews](https://talkingbiznews.com/media-news/travel-news-site-skift-has-laid-off-eight-staffers/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; [Travel Weekly AU](https://travelweekly.com.au/tripadvisor-fires-150-staff-and-contractors/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established) — economics favor scale and squeeze solo builders.

**Overall read:** global/English is the biggest, highest-value audience but also the most saturated, most AI/algorithm-exposed, and most consolidated — the hardest place for a solo bootstrapper to win on generic SEO travel content. Full detail + conflicts in `market-global.md`.

## Competitive Landscape & Profitable Niches

> Directional conclusions below are Medium–High confidence; nearly all specific dollar/traffic figures are Low confidence (self-reported income reports, third-party estimators like Latka/SimilarWeb, or dated 2016–2019 press). Full citations + a 10-row comparison table in `competitors-niches.md`.

**The landscape is barbelled.** At the top sit corporate roll-ups: The Points Guy and Lonely Planet are both owned by Red Ventures (Lonely Planet acquired December 2020, reportedly ~$50M), and the points/miles vertical runs largely as subdomains of the BoardingArea network ([Red Ventures press release](https://redventures.com/press/press-releases/red-ventures-acquires-lonely-planet), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Primary; [Detailed](http://detailed.com/travel-blogs/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). *The most lucrative keyword territory — credit-card and points content — is defended by corporate-backed teams with issuer deals and compliance, a bad place for a solo entrant to go head-on.* Beneath that is a long tail of independents whose realistic ceiling is low-single-thousands $/month; travel content sites trade on Empire Flippers at ~27–35x monthly profit (mid-five-figure exits) ([Empire Flippers listings](https://empireflippers.com/marketplace/affiliate-businesses-for-sale/travel/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low — asking prices, seller-reported).

**Revenue hierarchy by niche.** The clearest hierarchy runs on affiliate commission size, not audience size: points/miles/credit cards (highest commission, most saturated and defended) > luxury/adventure > generic destination/budget/van-life/family (display-ad-led, Mediavine RPM ~$10–15, frequently ~85% of revenue from ads, thin and saturated) ([Travelpayouts](https://www.travelpayouts.com/blog/travel-niches-to-grow-your-travel-blog/amp/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low; [Ashley Hajimirsadeghi](https://www.ashleyhajimirsadeghi.com/blog/what-i-made-year-mediavine-2024), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). Visa/digital-nomad monetizes best via an attached **service**, not content — SafetyWing (nomad health insurance) is the proof point, with Latka listing 2024 revenue at $34.1M (up from $20.3M in 2023) on a $195M Series B ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/safetywing), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low; [InsurTech Insights](https://www.insurtechinsights.com/safetywing-raises-35-million-to-scale-its-health-insurance-cover-for-remote-workers/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established — funding). *All niche-earnings numbers are self-reported, survivorship-biased, Low confidence.*

**What wins.** Four patterns recur across the standout cases: (1) **monetization intent beats audience size** (cards or recurring subscriptions, not pageview ads); (2) **tools/databases beat articles** — Rome2Rio (routing DB), seats.aero/PointsYeah (award-seat DBs), Nomad List (city DB), and Wanderlog (itinerary tool) each own a structured dataset answering a transactional question; (3) **programmatic SEO at scale** (one template × a database of rows — Tripadvisor's archetype, with India's Holidify cited around 3.6M monthly organic visits, headline figures conflicted and unverified, [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/programmatic-seo-real-world-examples-insights-433490), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; [UpGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/how-holidify-programmatic-seo-delivers-20m-monthly-organic-traffic/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low); (4) **brand/community for retention**. A standout bootstrapped model is the flight-deal freemium newsletter: Scott's Cheap Flights (now Going.com) reported 233,660 subscribers and $963,234 in post-promo sales as far back as 2016 with ~80% trial-to-paid conversion — a recurring subscription wrapped around a transactional, time-sensitive signal rather than SEO articles ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2016/11/29/how-a-29-year-old-turned-an-obsession-with-cheap-plane-tickets-into-a-1-million-business-in-under-2-years.html), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established — but dated).

**AI-search damage is concentrated on informational content.** Bloomberg's analysis found travel sites took "the most pronounced drops," and the well-known case is The Planet D (~90% traffic loss, stopped publishing) ([PPC Land summarizing Bloomberg/NPR](https://ppc.land/googles-ai-search-overhaul-decimates-website-traffic/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). The resilience signal points consistently toward transactional/tool intent: BrightEdge reports informational intent is ~71% of AI-Overview-cited volume and ~92% on ChatGPT — the queries AI answers — while transactional is just 2–3% of cited volume, so transactional/tool queries still route to real sites ([BrightEdge](https://help.brightedge.com/resources/weekly-ai-search-insights/ai-search-query-intent-evolution), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low — vendor). Destination-marketing sources warn that attractions "not connected to live booking platforms" risk being ignored by AI ([Blooloop](https://blooloop.com/technology/opinion/ai-search-travel/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). *This indicates that live, structured, bookable data is the defensible layer; static prose is the exposed one.*

**The directory/aggregator angle is favored over a blog for this builder**, on four grounds: it answers transactional/lookup queries AI doesn't kill; it offers programmatic scale from one dataset (matching a Supabase/directory skillset); it has a clean monetization stack (premium/claimed listings + booking commissions + Pro subscription, proven by Diveboard, PADI Dive Guides, Wanderlog, seats.aero, PointsYeah); and it is solo-operable (Nomad List proves it). **Caveats:** cold-start data acquisition is the hard part, and Nomad List's apparent decline — Latka pegs 2023 at ~$704K while 2024 sources converge on ~$47K MRR — shows directories plateau without data freshness plus an attached higher-value service ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/nomad-list), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low; [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/from-google-sheets-to-3-6m-as-a-solo-founder-de782f722a), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). AI trip-planners are the fastest-growing competitor to any planner tool, so even a tool must own proprietary or frequently-refreshed data to stay defensible.

**Whitespace.** The research points to: (1) fragmented, frequently-changing transactional data (award seats, multimodal routing, visa-rules-by-nationality, van-stopover/campsite databases); (2) under-served verticals with thin published competition — **surf camps returned zero directory players**, plus food tourism and non-English/geographic markets (cf. Holidify in India); and (3) broken/outdated incumbents (dive directories are 2013-era and sparse; Rome2Rio shows no fresh public development since Omio acquired it in 2019). **Thailand/SEA specifically:** only independent content blogs surfaced (Adventure in You reported ~$2K/mo by end of year one; Live Less Ordinary; The Thaiger; Travel Happy), with Chiang Mai/Bangkok/Phuket named as nomad hubs and visa/cost-of-living queries recurring ([Adventure in You](https://www.adventureinyou.com/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low; [Worldpackers](https://www.worldpackers.com/articles/digital-nomads-thailand), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). **No SEA-specific directory/aggregator tool with published metrics was found — a whitespace signal** given the large nomad population.

## Trends, Threats & Downside

> Weighted to recent data with a bias toward surfacing downside. Many Primary sources (Pew, SparkToro, Google Search Central) were reached via Established secondary carriers. Full citations in `threats-trends.md`.

**(a) AI-search traffic hit — High confidence on direction.** According to Pew Research (July 2025 study of 68,879 real Google searches in March 2025), when an AI Overview appeared, users clicked a traditional result on only 8% of visits versus 15% without one — roughly halving click-through — and clicked a link *inside* the summary on only ~1% of visits ([The Register — Pew](https://www.theregister.com/2025/07/22/google_ai_overviews_suppress_search/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established carrying Primary Pew data; [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/pew-google-ai-overviews-39808.html), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established). At the publisher level, Chartbeat data in the Reuters Institute's 2026 trends report shows **global Google referrals to publishers down ~33% in the year to November 2025 (US −38%)**, with surveyed media leaders expecting a further 43% average drop over three years. Critically, **AI assistants are not recapturing the loss — ChatGPT is 0.02% of referral traffic and Perplexity 0.002%** ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media-audience-and-business-data/google-traffic-down-2025-trends-report-2026/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established). AI Overviews spread fast — from ~6.5% of queries in January 2025 to ~30% of US desktop by September 2025 (Semrush, via multiple carriers). *No clean travel-sector-specific AI-traffic percentage exists* — a gap; travel-specific evidence is the HCU case material below.

**(b) HCU "devastation" — the best-documented threat.** The canonical case is HouseFresh (first-party/Primary): ~4,000→~200 daily Google visitors (a ~95% collapse) after the September 2023 HCU, deepening on the March 2024 core update ([HouseFresh](https://housefresh.com/how-google-decimated-housefresh/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High for the site's own numbers, tier: Primary — but the aggrieved party). The mechanism is Primary and important: with the March 2024 core update, Google folded the Helpful Content system into core ranking, turning it from a periodic classifier into an always-on, site-wide signal, so a tranche of "unhelpful" pages can drag down a whole domain ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-released-massive-search-quality-improvements-with-march-2024-core-update-and-multiple-spam-updates-438144), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established, quoting Google directly). Winners of the churn were Reddit, Wikipedia, and big brands — exactly what a new site cannot be. Travel-specific names are vendor-sourced and **Low confidence** (Culture Trip −70%, PlanetWare −90%, The Crazy Tourist ~−100%, [Travelpayouts](https://www.travelpayouts.com/blog/hcu-and-other-google-updates/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low), and the widely-cited "671 travel publishers / 213 lost >90%" figure could not be traced to a primary methodology (Low).

**(c) Zero-click.** SparkToro/Datos put US zero-click at 58.5% in 2024, rising to ~68% in 2026 ([via aggregators](https://www.elitecontentmarketer.com/zero-click-search-statistics/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established underlying / Low carrier). *Conflict (not resolved):* a narrower Datos desktop-only measure says ~27% because it counts only session-ends-without-forwarding — so "68%" and "27%" describe different things and must not be mixed or averaged.

**(d) Display RPM — a contrarian finding.** The skeptical read cuts *against* the doom narrative on price. Broad programmatic display pricing has not collapsed (digital display eCPM +3.4% YoY in Q2 2024, [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/guideline-ecpm-pricing-data-h2-2024), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established), and blogger Mediavine/Raptive RPMs are roughly flat-to-up (~$25–35, [thisweekinblogging](https://thisweekinblogging.com/mediavine-vs-raptive/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). **The revenue threat is VOLUME (pageviews), not unit price** — a stable ~$25–35 RPM applied to traffic that has fallen 33–95% still means revenue collapse — and new sites cannot access premium networks until they hit traffic minimums.

**(e) Channel risk on three fronts.** A travel site is exposed on three dependent channels at once: Google was still ~64% of traffic even for recovered sites (HouseFresh, a continuing single-point-of-failure); Amazon Associates was restructured in 2026 with cuts up to 50% and an April 14, 2026 change limiting commission to the same ASIN ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/amazon-associates-affiliate-rate-cuts-publishers/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established); and Facebook referrals were down ~43% vs May 2023 while Pinterest now sends <0.5% of referrals ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media-audience-and-business-data/media_metrics/publisher-traffic-sources-2019-2025/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium-High, tier: Established; [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reckon-with-declining-facebook-referral-traffic-as-the-platform-pulls-away-from-news/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). *The lesson generalizes: affiliate and referral terms are set unilaterally by third parties whose incentives are not aligned with a small publisher's survival.*

**(f) Most AI-substitutable = informational queries.** No travel-specific trigger study surfaced, so this is inference from general intent data plus the mechanics of AI answers (confidence: Low–Medium). Informational queries trigger AI Overviews far more than any other intent (~57–91% depending on period) while transactional triggers them least (~14–16%) ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-seo-statistics/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/guide/google-ai-overviews), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). *Applied to travel,* the classic SEO staples — "best time to visit X," "things to do in X," "how to get from X to Y," "is X safe," "packing list" — are exactly the fact-seeking, question-format informational queries AI answers in the SERP, so they are the **most exposed**; booking-intent queries, interactive tools, personal narrative, and fresh/hyper-local first-hand info are the **least exposed**.

**(g) Structural risks.** Travel earnings are seasonal and lumpy (a "September slump," weak January), macro-shock-sensitive (COVID being the precedent), demand constant upkeep as prices/visa-rules/hours go stale, and sit in a crowded niche now full of AI-generated spam.

**(h) The contrarian/viable path.** The downside is real but not total. HouseFresh fully recovered on October 11, 2025 (~2 years 1 month after the collapse) — described as the only widely-verified full return to pre-HCU levels — and crucially it had *diversified* during the crisis, building a ~30,000-subscriber YouTube channel ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/housefresh-achieves-notable-traffic-recovery-after-google-algorithm-impacts-2/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). The survivor moat is first-hand, demonstrably-experienced content aligned with E-E-A-T ([Amsive](https://www.amsive.com/insights/seo/how-googles-latest-helpful-content-system-update-is-impacting-serps/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established), plus email (one guide reports 41% of marketers call it their most effective channel), video (Expedia 2025: 71% say video influenced destination choice, vendor-cited), community, and treating Google as one channel among several ([Skift](https://skift.com/2025/02/03/how-has-ai-changed-travel-planning/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). **Synthesis:** most-exposed = a brand-less Google-organic informational listicle on display ads + Amazon affiliate with no email/video (the classic 2018 programmatic-SEO travel-blog playbook); most-defensible = branded, first-hand, direct/email/video-diversified, with tools/booking-intent pages.

## Build Feasibility — Solo, Vanilla JS + Supabase

> Strong Primary coverage on the load-bearing technical and legal claims (Google Search Central, Supabase docs, FTC, Google Maps pricing, Mediavine help page); API/commission figures are mostly Low-tier and flagged. Full detail + a 7-item compliance checklist in `build-feasibility.md`.

**(a) Programmatic SEO still works — but the bar rose sharply.** The pattern (generate many pages from structured rows) is the textbook use case for travel/geo directories; Nomad List is the exemplar (~33,000 pages, ~43.2K monthly organic from two keyword templates filled from a database, [upGrowth teardown](https://upgrowth.in/how-nomadlist-programmatic-seo-delivers-43-2k-monthly-organic-traffic/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low — third-party estimate). The deciding factor is **unique, substantive per-page data**, not page count or templating; the dominant failure mode is the "thin-content trap" ("Google indexes 50 pages then stops" on near-duplicates), which is also the main HCU penalty risk ([Dirstarter](https://dirstarter.com/blog/programmatic-seo-directory-websites), [Best SEO Singapore](https://bestseo.sg/blog/programmatic-seo-when-to-use), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). *Confidence that the pattern works in principle: Medium; that any given thin implementation will rank: Low (it likely won't).*

**(b) Data sources — a major 2026 finding.** According to PhocusWire, **Amadeus shut down its Self-Service for Developers portal on July 17, 2026** (new registrations paused, existing API keys disabled), leaving only gatekept Enterprise access — effectively off the table for bootstrappers ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/amadeus-shut-down-self-service-apis-portal-developers), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established — trade press). The viable solo stack is **OSM/Overture/Foursquare + Geoapify** for POI seed data ingested into Supabase — OpenStreetMap's ODbL requires attribution ("OpenStreetMap contributors") and carries share-alike obligations, and OSM explicitly does not offer a free production API for third parties ([OSM Copyright/ODbL](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Primary). Google Places is usable only for display-time enrichment within free caps, because its paid rates are steep (Place Details Pro $17/1,000; Nearby/Text Search Pro $32/1,000) and — critically — its terms **bar long-term caching of content**, which collides with the "ingest once, render thousands of static pages" model ([Google Maps Platform pricing](https://mapsplatform.google.com/pricing/), [Google Maps core services pricing](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Primary). Viator/GetYourGuide are the monetization APIs but all commission figures are Low-tier (Viator ~8%; GetYourGuide cited at 5% / 5.25% / 8%, conflicting) and official portals were unreachable ([Affilimate](https://affilimate.com/programs/viator-affiliate-program/), [Tradedoubler](https://directory.tradedoubler.com/en/programs/362232-getyourguide-US), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low).

**(c) SSR/SSG for SEO — the central technical question.** Supabase's own docs describe it as a backend-as-a-service with **no SSR/SEO/HTML-generation layer** ([Supabase architecture](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/getting-started/architecture), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Primary), so the SEO burden falls entirely on the frontend. Google officially renders and indexes JavaScript, but its own guidance says "server-side or pre-rendering is still a great idea" because not all bots run JS and app-shell sites ship near-empty initial HTML ([Google Search Central: JavaScript SEO basics](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Primary; [web.dev](https://web.dev/javascript-and-google-search-io-2019), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). *A pure vanilla-JS client-rendered SPA is the highest-risk choice for a thousands-of-pages directory* — technically indexable, but the two-phase render queue, crawl-budget limits, and soft-404 client-routing problems bite hardest exactly at that scale. **Recommendation:** pair Supabase with **Astro or Next.js SSG/ISR** (build-time generation, using incremental/batched builds past ~5,000 pages) or bolt on a prerendering service (~$49/mo). *This means the "pure vanilla JS" preference should bend toward a static-generation layer for the SEO-dependent pages.* Confidence: High (Google Primary + multiple corroborating sources).

**(d) Legal/compliance (FTC Primary/High).** Affiliate disclosure must be clear and conspicuous and placed *beside* the link: a full sentence or "Paid link" is acceptable, but "affiliate link" alone, a homepage-only disclosure, and a hyperlinked disclosure are **not** adequate ([FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking), [FTC Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Primary). The FTC Rule on Consumer Reviews (16 CFR Part 465) took effect October 21, 2024 and bans fake/incentivized reviews — relevant if the directory hosts user reviews ([Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/advertising-law/an-in-depth-look-at-the-ftcs-updates-2), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). For EU/UK ad serving, a Google-certified CMP implementing Consent Mode v2 has been mandatory since January 2024, and Thailand's PDPA requires opt-in for non-essential cookies ([Chambers Thailand PDPA](https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/view/158325293/464), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). AdSense has no traffic minimum, but "low-value content" is its top rejection reason — reinforcing the thin-content warning from (a).

**(e) Cost and timeline.** Infrastructure is cheap — **$0–50/month** (Supabase free/$25, static hosting, ~$12 domain, CMP, optional prerender). Monetization thresholds moved favorably: **Raptive dropped to 25,000 monthly pageviews (effective ~October 20, 2025)** ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/raptive-drops-traffic-requirement-by-75-to-25000-views/558780/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established), and Mediavine's official page now lists Journey at ≥1,000 sessions and Official at ≥$5,000/yr ad revenue, making the widely-quoted "50,000 sessions" figure legacy ([Mediavine](https://help.mediavine.com/what-does-it-take-to-get-approved-by-mediavine), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). **The binding bottleneck is TIME to reach traffic, not build cost** — reaching 25,000 monthly pageviews on organic for a brand-new domain typically takes many months to well over a year, and **no reliable "time-to-25k-pageviews" benchmark was found** (the single most important unknown for the go/no-go).

**(f) Non-Google traffic (all Low-tier).** The standard low-cost diversification stack for travel is Pinterest (treated as a visual search engine, the highest-leverage non-Google channel), short-form video repurposing, Flipboard, owned email, and genuine community posting (Reddit/forums), plus Stay22/Travelpayouts as affiliate-aggregator distribution ([Travelpayouts Pinterest](https://travelpayouts.com/blog/how-to-use-pinterest-to-promote-a-travel-blog/), [Stay22 community](https://community.stay22.com/diversifying-traffic-sources-for-travel-bloggers-beyond-algorithms-in-2026), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). None of these reliably replaces Google, and a new site cold-starts on every platform.

## Key Findings

**1. The obvious model — a travel blog monetized by display ads + affiliate via Google SEO — is the single worst thing to build in 2026.** Every axis converged on this. AI Overviews and zero-click search (~68% of US searches end click-free per SparkToro, and AI assistants recapture almost none of it — ChatGPT ~0.02% of referrals per Chartbeat/Reuters Institute) plus the Helpful Content Update have structurally gutted informational travel content: documented 70–95% traffic losses (HouseFresh, PlanetWare, The Planet D, which shut down) and even flagships shrinking (The Points Guy −21% YoY, Nomadic Matt −48% YoY, SimilarWeb estimates). This is a structural shift, not a dip (High confidence on direction; specific magnitudes Low–Medium, self-reported/estimator-sourced).

**2. Geography decides monetization by roughly 10x.** English-speaking Western (and Indian) inbound readers earn Western-tier display RPMs (~$15–35 travel) plus high-value affiliate bookings; a Thai-domestic audience earns a fraction (YouTube CPM ~$1.30 vs US ~$14.67; Thai display RPM <$1). The decisive corollary: Chinese tourists — the biggest and most volatile inbound segment — are effectively unreachable through an independent English/Thai site (they live inside Xiaohongshu/Douyin/Ctrip). *This suggests* the money-making audience is English-language inbound (Western + Indian + nomad + premium), never Thai-domestic or Chinese. (RPM figures all Low-tier, but the ~10x direction is consistent across every source.)

**3. Within the collapsing landscape, one layer is defensible: transactional/tool/lookup intent backed by live, structured data — a directory or tool, not articles.** AI answers mostly informational queries (71–92% of AI-cited volume per BrightEdge), while transactional queries are only ~2–3% of the volume AI answers cite. AI rarely stands in for transactional pages, so those pages still get the click. *The pattern across the competitive and threats axes indicates* that "live, bookable, structured data" is the defensible moat, and it happens to match the builder's existing directory/Supabase skillset.

**4. But directories have their own failure mode, and ad/affiliate is fragile.** Directories cold-start hard (data acquisition) and plateau without data freshness plus an attached higher-value service (Nomad List is often cited as a cautionary case, but its revenue is unverified: estimators range from ~$47K MRR (≈$560K/yr) to $3–5M/yr, and comparing Latka's ~$704K/yr with a ~$47K MRR figure mixes units, so no clear decline is established — review 2026-10-23). Separately, direct OTA affiliate terms can be withdrawn unilaterally. In Booking.com's May 2025 "Bookinggeddon", thousands of small partners lost their direct deals on 30 days' notice and were moved to networks (CJ/Awin) (Skift, Established). *This argues for* aggregators (Travelpayouts) over direct-OTA dependence, and for diversification.

**5. The money that survives is owned-audience and service revenue, not RPM.** Across the monetization and threats axes the resilient streams are email lists, communities/memberships, info products, and services (trip planning $300–$580+; SafetyWing insurance ~$34M revenue in 2024 shows the nomad-service demand). *The synthesis conclusion:* treat search/social as top-of-funnel feeding an owned audience, not as the monetization pipe itself. Build cost is trivial ($0–50/mo); the binding constraint is TIME to reach traffic.

## Market Pick — Thai vs Inbound vs Global

The research decisively ranks the three options the user left open.

- **Thai-domestic — reject for a money-making bet.** Largest traffic (~202M trips/yr) but roughly one-tenth the revenue per visitor and thin local affiliate. Rational only if money is *not* the primary goal, or if paired with a local commerce/service model.
- **Global/English generic — reject as a generic play.** The biggest, highest-RPM audience, but the most saturated, most AI-exposed, and most consolidated (Red Ventures et al.); a solo builder loses head-on.
- **Inbound-to-Thailand/SEA, English-language, narrow niche, tool/data-shaped — GO.** This is the sweet spot: it captures Western/Indian RPMs, has confirmed whitespace (no SEA nomad/vertical directory with published metrics was found), exploits the builder's likely Thailand proximity and local knowledge, is too small for the big incumbents to bother with, and — if built around transactional/lookup data — is the most AI-resilient shape available.

**Recommendation:** build in **English, for inbound/nomad/Indian + premium audiences, as a narrow data/tool-driven directory over a specific Thailand/SEA vertical, with an owned-audience + service/affiliate layer.** Reach is global (English); the edge is Thailand/SEA depth. This is *not* a generic "promote Thailand tourism" blog and *not* a Thai-language domestic site.

## Ranked Project Ideas (with go/no-go)

1. **Thailand/SEA nomad + visa-logistics hub (directory + tools + service) — GO (strongest).** DTV visa guidance, a visa-rules-by-nationality lookup, cost-of-living and nomad-base data (Chiang Mai/Bangkok/Phuket), and a coworking/café/long-stay accommodation database. Monetize with an attached paid service (visa/relocation help), travel-insurance affiliate (SafetyWing/Genki), eSIM (up to ~20% via Klook), Agoda long-stay, and an email list. *Why:* confirmed whitespace, high-intent evergreen queries, and the service + insurance streams are both the best-monetizing and the most AI-resilient — all for an English audience. *Risk:* cold-start data; Nomad List owns the generic global slot, so go SEA-specific and deeper. Evidence: competitors-niches + market-thailand axes.

2. **A single underserved vertical directory where incumbents are dead or absent — GO (conditional).** Dive sites (existing directories are ~2013-era and sparse) or surf camps (zero directory players found), scoped SEA-first then global; bookable/transactional, monetized via GetYourGuide/Viator (~8%), dive insurance, and claimed/premium listings. *Risk:* smaller TAM; must validate demand and data availability first.

3. **Transactional lookup tool for fragmented, frequently-changing data — GO (conditional, pick carefully).** Visa-rules-by-nationality for SEA is the best candidate (evergreen, high-intent, liability-sensitive and therefore AI-answer-resistant). Avoid multimodal routing (Rome2Rio/Omio and Google own it, and it is data-heavy).

4. **Generic English "promote Thailand tourism" content blog — NO-GO as the primary bet.** The most-exposed model (informational + Google-dependent + saturated + consolidated). Viable only as a thin first-hand-experience/E-E-A-T content layer feeding idea #1 or #2's owned audience.

5. **Thai-language domestic travel site — NO-GO for money.** Roughly one-tenth the RPM and thin affiliate. Reconsider only with a local-service/commerce angle.

> **Go/no-go headline (honest):** a money-making tourism website is viable **only** in a narrow, non-obvious, English, tool/data + owned-audience form. The default form most people build — a travel blog — is a bad bet in 2026.

## Risks and Uncertainties

- **Evidence quality:** nearly all dollar and traffic figures in this report are Low confidence — self-reported income reports, third-party estimators (SimilarWeb/Latka/Semrush), or vendor/aggregator pages. No official affiliate-program terms were reachable. Verify commission rates on live partner pages before modeling revenue.
- **Unresolved conflicts (not silently resolved):** online-travel market size varies ~3x by definition ($94B OTA vs $560–670B broad vs ~$1T bookings); AI-Overview CTR impact ranges from −34.5% (Ahrefs) to −61% (Seer) by study (not comparable); zero-click measured at ~27% (Datos desktop) vs ~68% (SparkToro) — not comparable, do not average; Mediavine threshold (legacy 50K sessions vs official 1K sessions / $5K revenue); 2025 Thai domestic trips 202.37M (Ministry) vs 206.63M (Khaosod); Viator 8% vs 10%; GetYourGuide 5%/5.25%/8%; Thai PDPA fines THB 5M vs 10M; The Planet D traffic drop ~90% (NPR) vs ~50% (UCLA).
- **Platform risk is real and demonstrated:** Booking.com cut affiliates unilaterally (May 2025); Amazon Associates cut rates up to 50% (2026); Google was still ~64% of even recovered sites' traffic.
- **Directory-specific failure modes:** cold-start data acquisition, and the plateau-without-service pattern (Nomad List).
- **Compliance (mandatory, not optional):** FTC affiliate disclosure (clear, beside the link), the FTC Review Rule (if hosting reviews), EU/UK Consent Mode v2 CMP, and Thai PDPA opt-in.
- **Key gaps:** no travel-affiliate TAM; no reliable global travel-search-volume figure; no inbound-English-blog-usage survey; no travel-specific AI-Overview trigger study (section (f) of threats is inference); and — most important — **no "time-to-25k-pageviews" benchmark**, which is the single biggest unknown for the go/no-go since time-to-traffic is the binding constraint.

## Next Steps

1. **Validate before building (highest priority).** Pick one niche (idea #1 recommended) and run a keyword + competitor-gap check: are there enough low-difficulty (KD<30) transactional/lookup queries with page-one gaps, and is the underlying data obtainable and legally reusable (ODbL/OSM, provider terms)? Use `customer-research` + `seo-audit` + `competitors`.
2. **Confirm the data source + licensing** for the chosen vertical (OSM/Overture/Foursquare/Geoapify into Supabase; note Amadeus Self-Service shut down July 2026, and Google Places bars long-term caching).

4. **Design the owned-audience + service layer first** (email capture, the paid service or premium listing) — that is where the money survives, so it should shape the whole product.
