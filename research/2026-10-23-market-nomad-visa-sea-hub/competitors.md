## Competitive Teardown

This axis maps who already serves the nomad / visa / relocation / city-info needs of English-speaking remote workers in Thailand and wider Southeast Asia (SEA), how each monetizes, how fresh their data is, and where a solo builder could carve defensible whitespace. The headline finding, developed throughout and flagged explicitly at the end, is that **no structured, maintained SEA-specific nomad+visa directory/tool exists** — the category splits cleanly into (1) one aging global city-data tool (Nomad List), (2) transactional visa/relocation service firms, (3) well-funded insurance incumbents now pushing into visa concierge, and (4) a long tail of unstructured blogs. That structural gap is the core opportunity.

### (a) Nomad List / nomads.com — the incumbent "city data" tool, aging

Nomad List (rebranded to the domain nomads.com) is the reference product in this space: a solo-built, crowdsourced ranking of cities for remote workers scoring cost of living, internet speed, safety, weather, and visa notes, plus a paid community (Telegram chat, meetups, dating/friend matching, "Replay" travel tracking, a 183-day residence/tax-threshold alert tool, climate finder, and a jobs feed). Its official pricing page lists a **one-time Lite Membership at $19.99 and a Lifetime Membership**, both one-time with a 7-day money-back guarantee, plus a free tier with limited city data for 1,500+ cities ([nomads.com/pricing](https://nomads.com/pricing), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). Note the pricing page rendered ambiguously — it showed prices of $19.99 and $1 without cleanly pairing them to tiers (the $1 is almost certainly a promotional/trial figure, not the real lifetime price), so exact current tier pricing is **Low confidence** and should be re-verified at signup.

On scale and monetization, Nomad List is a membership business run by solo founder Pieter Levels with no staff, using AI tooling ([Pieter.com help center](https://ideasbugs.featurebase.app/help/articles/9570846-who-built-and-is-behind-interior-ai), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low). Revenue estimates cluster around **~$700K ARR / ~$47K MRR**: Latka reports $704.2K in 2023 revenue (up from $505.1K in 2021) ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/nomad-list), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low), an indie-hacking Substack profile cites "roughly $47,000 MRR, down slightly from its peak" ([Indie Hackerman Substack](https://indiehackerman.substack.com/p/the-godfather-of-indie-hacking), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low), and a podcast summary repeats ~$700K ARR for Nomad List alone ([recall.it podcast summary](https://www.recall.it/summary/entrepreneurship/pieter-levels-programming-viral-ai-startups-and-digital-nomad-life-or-lex-fridman-podcast), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). Because all three are estimator/secondary sources, the figure is best treated as **~$500K–$800K ARR, confidence: Low–Medium**. One low-quality outlier claimed "$3–5M/year" and conflicts sharply with every other source; it should be disregarded.

**Reported decline / neglect:** The clearest signal is the Substack note that MRR "has come down slightly from its peak popularity and from when he shifted his focus towards his AI products" ([Indie Hackerman Substack](https://indiehackerman.substack.com/p/the-godfather-of-indie-hacking), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). Levels now splits attention across Photo AI (2023+) and Interior AI (launched 2022), both solo-run ([tooldirectory.ai](https://tooldirectory.ai/tools/interior-ai), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). I found **no hard 2024–2026 traffic series** for the domain: SimilarWeb (an estimator, treat as Low) showed nomads.com at **447.4K total visits over 3 months (~149K/mo), September 2026, up 31.3% MoM**, with no figure available for the legacy nomadlist.com domain ([SimilarWeb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/nomads.com/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). This *conflicts* with an older "250K unique visitors/month" profile figure ([Monolith/Beehiiv](https://monolith.beehiiv.com/p/nomad-list), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low) — likely a timing difference (the Monolith figure predates the decline, and SimilarWeb's "up 31%" is a short-term bounce off a lower base). Net: traffic is materially below its mid-2010s peak but not dead; **confidence: Low** on any precise number.

**Data freshness / SEA coverage weaknesses — the exploitable soft spot.** Nomad List covers SEA cities (Bangkok, Chiang Mai, Bali, etc.) inside its global dataset but the data is crowdsourced and criticized as stale and biased. A 2024 analysis argued its reliance on user contributions introduces bias and that its vetting methods "lack transparency" ([MightyTravels](https://www.mightytravels.com/2024/08/analyzing-nomadlist-a-critical-look-at-data-accuracy-for-digital-nomads-in-2024/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). Nomad List's own FAQ concedes "there will always be data points that are not accurate," that cost data is only "within the range of 90% of other cost-of-living sites," and that safety ratings are "highly discussed" and skewed by users being "overly positive and patriotic about their own city" ([nomads.com FAQ](https://nomads.com/faq/how-accurate-is-nomadscom-9634974), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). Crucially, **its visa data is user-contributed notes, not a maintained, Thailand-DTV/LTR/Elite-current legal layer** — exactly the data that changed most in 2024–2026. **Solo-beatable? Yes on the SEA+visa vertical specifically** — it is itself solo-built, its data is thin/stale for SEA visa logistics, and the founder is distracted by AI products. It is not beatable on global breadth or community network effects.

### (b) Visa-service & relocation sites — transactional, opaque, service-fee driven

This cluster sells *done-for-you* visa processing and relocation, monetizing via a service fee marked up over government fees (often blended so the split is unclear to the buyer).

**NomadsEmbassy** positions as a digital-nomad-visa blog + lead-gen hub: it offers a free "digital nomad visa eligibility check" and routes users to accommodation, insurance, and tax partners, with visa-guide pages across ~60 countries including a Thailand Digital Nomad Visa page. It is operated by NMDS OÜ (Estonia) and shows **no public pricing**, which is consistent with an affiliate/referral-commission model rather than direct service fees ([NomadsEmbassy](https://nomadsembassy.com/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). Strength: broad content SEO and a clean eligibility-quiz funnel. Weakness: thin, generic guidance ("informational purposes only"), no transactional depth, and no visible structured directory.

**iVisa** is the scaled intermediary — it handles visa/e-visa paperwork for 200+ nationalities and charges a **service fee on top of the government fee** (the fee varies by visa type and processing speed), a model one Trustpilot reviewer called misleading because optional third-party charges resemble official costs ([Trustpilot](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/ivisatravel.com/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low; business-model description corroborated by [Issa Compass](https://www.issacompass.com/insights/thailand-visa-service-pricing-decoded-what-platform-fees-hidden-charges-and-chea), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). iVisa claims 526% revenue growth 2021–2024 (Deloitte Technology Fast 500 #155), "millions of applications a year," 2M+ cumulative customers, and bootstrapped growth; it is merging with HelloGov AI (effective July 2026) to process a projected 1.5M+ applications/year ([iVisa blog](https://www.ivisa.com/es/blog/ivisa-emerges-as-a-travel-tech-leader), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low — all company-reported). Latka has no revenue estimate for iVisa. iVisa focuses on short-stay tourist/e-visas, **not the long-stay DTV/Elite/LTR logistics that SEA nomads actually need** — a coverage gap.

**Siam Legal International** (Bangkok) is a licensed legal consultancy and a **certified General Sales & Service Agent (GSSA) of the Thailand Privilege Card Co.** (the Elite visa operator), and it processes DTV, LTR, and Elite applications. Reported Thailand Privilege/Elite tiers include Gold at ฿900,000 for 5 years, with Bronze tiers reported inconsistently at ฿600,000–฿650,000 ([barchart/press release](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/34706067/thailand-elite-visa-expands-membership-packages-for-long-term-thailand-residency), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; Siam Legal's GSSA status noted in same source). Siam Legal's own *service-fee markup* on DTV/LTR was not published in my results. DTV is reported at ฿10,000 per issuance (+฿10,000 for an in-country 180-day extension) and LTR government fees around ฿50,000 one-time, with all-in agent packages around ฿85,000 ([Issa Compass LTR cost](https://www.issacompass.com/insights/the-real-cost-of-a-thailand-ltr-visa-government-fees-service-fees-and-what-to-bu), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). Sources disagree on exact fees (see Conflicts). Relocation concierges are fragmented and expensive: an independent Chiang Mai operator charges a **$400 flat relocation-coordination fee** for a 45-day window (housing search, lease negotiation, utilities) ([Pensight listing](https://pensight.com/x/ebonytempleluxtravel/meeting-f026bbb3-a390-4d9e-9288-8e787927fc8e), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low), while enterprise firms (Pacific Orientation, Asian Tigers — 16,000 families/yr, Crown Relo) serve corporate HR, not individuals ([Expat Arrivals](https://www.expatarrivals.com/asia-pacific/thailand/relocation-companies-thailand), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). **Solo-beatable? The service execution (lawyers, GSSA licensing) is NOT solo-beatable, but the information/comparison/orchestration layer above these providers is wide open** — nobody neutrally aggregates "which Thai visa fits you, what it really costs all-in, and which vetted agent to use."

### (c) Insurance players — "competitors for attention & affiliate," and now entering visa/relocation

Nomad insurers matter here two ways: they compete for the same high-intent affiliate clicks (insurance is the richest affiliate payout in this niche), and one of them is **directly invading the visa/relocation space**.

**SafetyWing** is the category leader. Latka estimates **2024 revenue ~$34.1M (up from $20.3M in 2023), ~25K customers** ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/safetywing), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low), and it raised a **$35M Series B led by Kinnevik in April 2022 (~$195M valuation), ~$46–47M total funding** ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/250065/safetywing-raises-35m-in-series-b/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; corroborated by [Coverager](https://coverager.com/safetywing-raises-35-million/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). Nomad Insurance (underwritten via WorldTrips/Tokio Marine HCC) is priced around **Essential ~$56–63 per 4 weeks for ages 18–39 and Complete ~$177/mo** (rising steeply with age; US coverage +~$53/4wk) — figures from nomad blogs, **confidence: Low–Medium**, verify at the official quote tool ([earthsims pricing guide](https://www.earthsims.com/insurance/safetywing-pricing-guide/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). **Most important strategic fact:** SafetyWing's non-profit arm **Plumia** launched the **Nomad Border Pass / "Nomad Citizen"** — a multi-country remote-work visa product offering **application assistance for 10+ nomad visas including Thailand**, priced **$400/mo (ages 18–39) and $600/mo (40–49), income requirement US$4,000/mo** ([SafetyWing Nomad Citizen FAQ](https://intercom.help/safetywing/en/articles/11088830-nomad-citizen-faq), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary; product page [safetywing.com/nomad-border-pass](https://safetywing.com/nomad-border-pass), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). This is a **$35M-funded incumbent moving into exactly the visa-concierge layer** a solo builder might target — the single biggest competitive threat in this study, though its subscription pricing ($400+/mo) is steep and leaves room for a cheaper self-serve information/tooling tier below it.

**Genki** competes on age-sensitive, health-only pricing — a 20-something EU/UK plan ~€52.50/mo vs SafetyWing ~$56.28, a 50-something ~€109.80 vs ~$145.04 — but excludes travel-disruption cover (no trip interruption, baggage, liability) and is cheaper for over-50s ([bankeronwheels comparison](https://www.bankeronwheels.com/genki-vs-world-nomads-vs-safetywings/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). **Insured Nomads** is smaller and more B2B2C: one seed round of ~$2.1M (March 2021), founded late 2019, grown with little VC; it sells a direct-to-consumer international private medical plan (Allianz Partners-underwritten, $250K coverage, from ~£60/mo) distributed via the Doerscircle freelancer network (~135K members), plus an enterprise/distributed-team channel ([fintech.global](https://fintech.global/?p=183974), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; funding from [Welcome to the Jungle](https://app.welcometothejungle.com/companies/Insured-Nomads), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). **Takeaway for a solo builder:** don't compete *as* an insurer; monetize insurance as the top affiliate line while owning the neutral comparison/decision content these players can't credibly provide about each other.

### (d) City / cost-of-living data sites — crowdsourced, stale, and thinning out

**Numbeo** is the dominant cost-of-living database: crowdsourced price submissions from **700,000+ contributors, 8M+ price data points, 10,000+ cities**, with each figure stamped with last-update date and contributor count ([Wikipedia→Numbeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numbeo), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low-for-stats; freshness-display corroborated by [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/travel/numbeo-helps-you-figure-the-cost-of-travel), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). Its weakness is well-known: crowdsourced data carries noise, tourist bias, and lag, and reviewers note figures "not always 100% correct" ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/numbeo-com/about/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). Numbeo monetizes via ads + a paid API, and critically **does nothing with visas or SEA-specific nomad context** — it's raw price data only. **Teleport** (Teleport Cities: income/cost/quality-of-life for 260 cities) was acquired by **MOVE Guides** (which later became **Topia**) and effectively wound down as a consumer product ([Estonian World](https://estonianworld.com/?p=23727), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established) — note the task's "Topia" framing is right directionally (MOVE Guides → Topia) but the acquirer at the time was MOVE Guides; the consumer Teleport tool is essentially defunct today. **This is a telling market signal: the "beautiful cost-of-living comparison site" model did not sustain as a standalone business** (one commentator flatly said Teleport "wasn't making any money"). A solo builder should treat cost-of-living data as a feature/hook, not the business — the business is the high-intent visa+relocation transaction layer on top.

### (e) SEA-specific nomad guides/directories/blogs — all blogs, no structured tool

Every SEA-focused resource I found is **editorial content (blogs/guides), not a structured, queryable directory or tool.** The Thaiger runs sponsored one-off articles ("what $1,000/mo buys in Chiang Mai") not a directory ([The Thaiger guides](https://thethaiger.com/guides/may), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). Long-form Chiang Mai/Bali guides come from Freaking Nomads, Two Wandering Soles, A Brother Abroad, SmartLocal, ThailandStarterKit, SoutheastAsiaBackpacker, Hostelworld — all narrative blogs, several carrying **out-of-date visa info** (one still claimed Thailand "has no digital nomad visa," ignoring the DTV) ([SmartLocal](https://thesmartlocal.com/thailand/chiang-mai-remote-working), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low; DTV correction via [ThailandStarterKit](https://www.thailandstarterkit.com/de/moving/digitaler-nomade-chiang-mai/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). **ExpatDen** is the closest thing to a structured monetized SEA product: a Thailand/SEA expat guide site with a **premium subscription ($39.99/yr) and Business tier ($159.99/yr, includes a lawyer/accountant call), plus sponsorship/advertising** (pitching 50,000+ monthly expats / 100,000+ pageviews, e.g. featuring an insurer inside insurance guides) ([ExpatDen plans](https://www.expatden.com/plans/all-plans/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary; [ExpatDen ads](https://expatden.com/ads/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary) — but it's still fundamentally paywalled articles, not a tool/directory. On coworking, the nearest things to directories are **access-pass apps** (OpenSpace Passport ~20 offices; Deskhop hourly/daily desks in TH/SG/MY/PH, launched ~July 2025) — booking products, not information directories ([Globetrender](https://globetrender.com/2025/07/08/deskhop-coworking-app-digital-nomads-asia/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). **No SEA-specific structured nomad+visa directory was found (see Gaps).**

### (f) AI trip/relocation planners entering the space — early, fragmented, unproven

A wave of AI relocation planners is emerging but is early-stage and mostly tiny/unverified. **ExpatSpark AI** builds an emigration roadmap from an 8-question diagnostic with visa-eligibility scoring, free tier + **€19/mo (or €14/mo annual)** ([peerpush](https://peerpush.com/p/expatspark-ai), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). **Nomados AI** (launched Sep 2, 2025) ranks destinations, compares visa options, and claims "lock in a visa" ([BetaList](https://betalist.com/startups/nomados-ai), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). **Plarelo** generates a task/paperwork plan from structured intake (also serves relocation pros); **Migroot** is a visa-application tracker ($19/mo or one-time $1,190–$2,990) ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/migroot/about), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low); "Nomad Planning" and SeasonSync/ClimateRoute are student/concept projects, not live businesses. **Analysis:** these validate demand for AI-assisted visa/relocation decisions but none is SEA-specialized, none combines live/accurate Thai-visa data with a vetted-service marketplace, and most are generic global roadmap generators competing on the same stale public data. The threat is the *category direction*, not any single incumbent — and SafetyWing/Plumia (see c) is the far better-resourced version of this bet.

### Whitespace / gaps a solo builder could own (analysis)

Synthesizing across (a)–(f): the exploitable whitespace is a **structured, continuously-maintained SEA-first nomad+visa tool** that does three things no incumbent combines — (1) an accurate, dated, Thailand/SEA-specific visa layer (DTV vs LTR vs Elite vs education/retirement, all-in real costs, eligibility logic) that Nomad List's crowdsourced notes and generic AI planners get wrong or stale; (2) SEA cost-of-living + neighborhood + coworking + internet data that is fresher and more granular than Nomad List's global crowdsource and more contextual than raw Numbeo; and (3) a **neutral affiliate/marketplace monetization** layer routing high-intent users to vetted visa agents (Siam Legal-type), insurers (SafetyWing/Genki affiliate), and relocation concierges — capturing the lead-gen value NomadsEmbassy only half-exploits and the comparison neutrality no service firm or insurer can credibly offer. The Teleport failure warns that cost-of-living data alone isn't a business; the business is the transaction layer. The SafetyWing/Plumia move ($400+/mo) warns the window is closing from the top — but also validates the demand and leaves a self-serve/information tier underneath it unserved. Nomad List's founder distraction and stale SEA/visa data make the incumbent beatable *on this vertical*.
