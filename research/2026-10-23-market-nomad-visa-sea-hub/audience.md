## Audience — Size, Segments & Voice

_Research axis: audience size, segments, willingness to pay, and voice-of-customer (VOC) pains for a money-making digital-nomad + visa-logistics hub for Thailand & SEA (English-language). All sources accessed 2026-10-23. Geographic/time scope: Thailand + SEA, 2024–2026, English-speaking nomads._

**Sampling-bias note up front:** Most VOC below is mined from Reddit mirrors (ask.in.th, which re-hosts Reddit and Facebook-group threads), DTV Facebook groups, and expat blogs. This population skews toward engaged, skeptical, problem-experiencing nomads (people with a smooth experience rarely post), and toward Western English-speakers. Russian, Chinese, and Indian nomads — who the quantitative data suggests are large segments — are under-represented in English-language forums. Treat VOC as directional signal on *language and recurring themes*, not as representative frequencies.

### (a) How big is the audience?

There is no authoritative count of digital nomads, and estimates span an order of magnitude — roughly 10 million to 100 million depending on definition and method. The single most-cited figure is "over 40 million globally," which traces to the WYSE Travel Confederation: in a January 2023 analysis WYSE said it expected the global number to top 40 million, growing to around 60 million by 2030, building on earlier estimates of ~35 million in 2022 ([WYSE Travel Confederation](https://www.wysetc.org/2023/01/growth-and-developments-in-the-digital-nomad-market-since-covid-19/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). Secondary roundups repeat this 40M→60M framing and add a "~45 million currently" figure ([FMC Group](https://fmcgroup.com/digital-nomad-statistics-worldwide/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low; [No Direction Home / Substack](https://nodirectionhome.substack.com/p/how-many-digital-nomads-are-there), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). Because two reasonable-independent sources converge on the 40M-order-of-magnitude, I rate the "~40 million global, growing toward 60M by 2030" claim **Medium–High**, but with a wide error band. Note the much larger "80 million+" figures circulate from nomads.com (Nomad List), whose own FAQ admits a 10M–100M range and says to "take this with a grain of salt" — that figure is built on a paid-community membership base and is aspirational, so I flag it **Low** ([nomads.com FAQ](https://nomads.com/faq/how-many-digital-nomads-are-there-in-the-world-6774378), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low).

The most methodologically rigorous series is MBO Partners' US-only "State of Independence" survey. Its 2024 report counted 27.7 million full-time independent workers (up 6.5%) and ~72.7 million Americans doing some independent work ([MBO Partners press release](https://www.mbopartners.com/blog/press/new-study-shows-rise-in-independent-by-choice-workers-over-traditional-employment), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). Secondary summaries put MBO's US *digital-nomad-specific* count at ~18.1 million (2024) rising to ~18.5 million (2025), though I could not confirm the 18.1M figure in MBO's own primary materials, so treat the nomad-specific slice as **Low–Medium**. Analysts caution MBO's online-survey method over-represents educated, higher-income workers ([gigeconomydata.org](https://gigeconomydata.org/research/data-sources/mbo-survey), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established).

How many target or are in Thailand/SEA specifically? **No sources found for a reliable headcount of nomads physically in Thailand.** The Tourism Authority of Thailand publishes no nomad tally. The best proxies are indirect: a 2023 Instagram-hashtag study ranked Thailand third globally (behind the US and Spain) by #digitalnomad social activity, with Thailand at ~124,729 tagged posts/mentions vs the US at ~210,664, and Bangkok the top Asia-Oceania city at ~20,247 #digitalnomad Instagram posts ([Bangkok Post](https://bangkokpost.com/business/general/2579164), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Established [but social-media proxy, not headcount]). The strongest signal of *demand* is Thailand's consistently top-tier ranking as a nomad destination (see segment below) and DTV uptake (next). My synthesis: Thailand/SEA is one of the two or three most-wanted nomad regions globally, so even conservative framings put the addressable English-speaking audience in the low-to-mid hundreds of thousands actively in-region at any time, with a much larger funnel of aspirants — but this is **inferred (Low)**, not measured.

### (b) Thailand DTV uptake since mid-2024, and LTR numbers

The Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) launched 15 July 2024: a 5-year multiple-entry visa, 180 days per entry, 10,000 baht government fee (~US$275–310), requiring proof of ~500,000 baht (~US$15,500) in reserves and no income test ([IMI Daily](https://www.imidaily.com/asia-pacific/thailands-digital-nomad-visa-surpasses-35000-applicants-in-its-first-year/), accessed 2026-10-23, pub. 2025-07-20, confidence: High, tier: Established).

On uptake, official numbers are thin and inconsistent — this is a key data gap. The firmest figure: **"more than 35,000 applicants" in the first year** (to ~July 2025), reported by both SCMP (at the one-year anniversary) and IMI Daily, though neither names the official source behind the 35,000, and this is *applicants*, not approvals ([IMI Daily](https://www.imidaily.com/asia-pacific/thailands-digital-nomad-visa-surpasses-35000-applicants-in-its-first-year/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; SCMP corroborates the same figure but its article is paywalled/403). An earlier, better-attributed data point: a Thai MFA interview reportedly gave **~11,000 DTVs approved through mid-November 2024** (relayed secondhand on a forum, not independently verifiable), with the same thread noting the MFA said there were "no goal or targets for 2024 or 2025" ([ask.in.th thread, Jan 2025](https://ask.in.th/question/what-are-the-latest-statistics-on-approved-digital-nomad-visas-dtv-in-thailand-and-how-do-the-numbers-compare-by-nationality), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). Beyond that, figures are blog/forum estimates: one 1-year-review blog claims "~150,000+ applications" by April 2026 (sourced to "immigration office leaks, agent estimates, and public statements" — explicitly unverified, and the blog discloses it was AI-assisted and carries affiliate links) ([ExpatLife.ai](https://expatlife.ai/blog/thailand-dtv-visa-1-year-review-2026-what-actually-works), accessed 2026-10-23, pub. 2026-04-27, confidence: Low, tier: Low). Thai immigration does not publish official rejection rates; the same blog *estimates* rejection climbing from <5% at launch to ~18–22% by April 2026 — unverified. **Confidence on DTV uptake overall: Low**, because no two independent reputable sources agree beyond the 35,000 first-year applicant figure. Note also a relevant trigger event: Thailand cut its 60-day visa-free entry to 30 days effective 15 September 2026 (Royal Gazette 31 Aug 2026), citing misuse of long-stay options for illegal work — which should push more serious long-stayers toward the DTV ([NST/Bernama](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/world/region/2026/09/1525294/thailand-scraps-60-day-visa-free-entry-reinstates-30-day-scheme), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; [Freaking Nomads](https://freakingnomads.com/news/thailand-officially-cuts-visa-free-stay-to-30-days/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low). A further document tightening took effect 31 Aug 2026 (mandatory criminal-record clearance, proof of residence) ([Thethaiger](https://thethaiger.com/visas/dtv/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established).

The LTR (Long-Term Resident) visa — the higher-net-worth cousin, run by the Board of Investment — has much better official data. BOI figures show the program reached **12,011 approved applicants over its first four years (to ~Oct 2026)**, generating an estimated 43 billion baht (~US$1.28B) in economic value; the largest category is "Wealthy Pensioners" (4,561), followed by "Dependants" (3,891), and top nationalities are the US (2,400+), Japan (1,300+), and the UK (1,000+) ([Nation Thailand / BOI](https://www.nationthailand.com/business/40071881), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; [The Star / BOI](https://www.thestar.co.uk/must-read/thailands-long-term-residence-visa-hits-12000-approvals-in-four-years-9267125), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established; [European Business Magazine](https://europeanbusinessmagazine.com/thailands-ltr-visa-hits-12000-approvals-in-four-years-adding-usd-1-28-billion-to-the-economy/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low). Intermediate snapshots: ~6,000+ by end-2024 (Europe 2,500, US 1,080, Japan 610, China 340, India 280), and 6,562 endorsed for 11 Sep 2022–28 Feb 2025 ([BOI via search roundup](https://osos.boi.go.th/EN/news/2158), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). My synthesis: LTR (~12K in 4 years) is a smaller, wealthier cohort; DTV is the higher-volume, lower-friction product that the mass nomad audience actually funnels into — and the one generating the most confusion and service demand.

### (c) Nationality/segment mix, income, and budgets

Globally, the nomad population skews Western: one study puts the US top at ~43% of origin, followed by the UK, Russia, Canada, and Germany ([Nebeus, via Travelling For Business](https://travellingforbusiness.co.uk/?p=12894), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low); a 2022 survey summary gives US 31%, Portugal 8%, Germany 7%, Brazil 5%, with 76% identifying as white ([Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/thailand/tourism/40021375), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Established). For the DTV specifically, reported top nationalities cluster on US, UK, Russia, and Germany (with Israel appearing by 2026) — but these come from blog/forum sources and conflict in ordering ([ExpatLife.ai](https://expatlife.ai/blog/thailand-dtv-visa-1-year-review-2026-what-actually-works), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). There is strong *anecdotal* signal of a very large **Russian** cohort in SEA — forum observers report Russians at "easily 95%" of a DTV queue at the Jakarta embassy and "~90%" at a Cambodian border crossing (single-location observations, not statistics) ([ask.in.th](https://ask.in.th/question/what-are-the-latest-statistics-on-approved-digital-nomad-visas-dtv-in-thailand-and-how-do-the-numbers-compare-by-nationality), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). One MFA-attributed (secondhand) top-5 for DTV was China, UK, US, Germany, Australia. **No sources found** quantifying Indian nomads in Thailand, despite India being newly added to the 30-day visa-exempt list. **Conflict flagged:** sources do not agree on DTV nationality ordering, and the China-vs-Russia-vs-US "number one" question is unresolved — all **Low** confidence.

On income and budgets the data is loose. Spending/cost estimates are the best-documented: Thailand's all-in monthly nomad cost is cited at ~$1,042 (Nebeus, ranking Thailand the most popular / 4th most affordable) ([Scandasia](https://scandasia.com/chiang-mai-named-worlds-cheapest-city-for-digital-nomads/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low), ~$1,360/month median (Nomada city data) ([Nomada.tools](https://www.nomada.tools/countries/thailand), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low), with the 2022 global average budget at ~$1,875/month. A Mahidol University academic study of 115 nomads in Thailand reported average accommodation spend of 14,976 baht/month ([Mahidol repository](https://repository.li.mahidol.ac.th/handle/123456789/114312), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). On *income*, estimates are far shakier: a secondhand claim attributes a "~$3,000/month average income" to a Thailand BOI study (original not located, **Low**), and one Thai article gives a comically wide "THB 20,000/month to USD 20,000/month" spread — underscoring that income is essentially un-pinned-down. Practical segmentation that emerges for the hub's purposes: (1) **budget nomads / long-stay backpackers** (THB 30,000–45,000/month, Chiang Mai-centric, price-sensitive, border-run culture); (2) **mid-market remote workers / freelancers** (the DTV core — need legitimacy, banking, coworking, willing to pay for done-for-you visa help); (3) **high-earner / LTR tier** (wealthy pensioners, executives, families — fewer in number, higher willingness to pay for white-glove relocation/tax). This segmentation is my synthesis from the spending data and visa-tier split, labeled **inference**.

Thailand's pull for this audience is well-evidenced: it ranks at or near the top of 2026 destination lists — Bangkok #1 and Chiang Mai #2 in a nomad.com community vote (Jan 2026), Thailand #3 globally on the Rumavi Global Relocation Index 2026 (behind Malaysia and Portugal), and Chiang Mai in Forbes' 2026 top-8 ([Khaosod English](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/news/2026/01/15/bangkok-tops-global-digital-nomad-rankings-chiang-mai-2nd/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; [Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40070951), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established).

### (d) Willingness to pay — what nomads already buy

This audience already spends on a stack of paid services, which validates monetization:

- **Visa agents/services** are the biggest and most contested spend. The DTV government fee is 10,000 baht, but agents charge wildly: document-only help commonly runs 5,000–25,000 baht (one user paid 16,000 baht including the visa fee; some advertise flat ~3,000 baht online-application help or ~4,990 baht document review), full agency/visa-run packages run 75,000–150,000 baht, and bank-balance "financial aid"/sponsor workarounds push quotes to 65,000–220,000 baht (one outlier quote of 260,000 baht was called out as unreasonable) ([Issa Compass](https://www.issacompass.com/insights/dtv-visa-total-cost-breakdown-government-fees-agent-charges-and-hidden-expenses-), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low; [Pattaya Mail](https://www.pattayamail.com/news/visa-companies-offer-trouble-free-dtv-applications-474522), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Established; [ask.in.th fee threads](https://ask.in.th/question/why-do-agents-charge-more-than-50000-baht-for-dtv-applications-when-the-official-visa-cost-is-much-lower), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). The large, opaque gap between the ~10K government fee and ~50K+ agent quotes is itself a loud VOC pain (below) and a clear content/service wedge.
- **Insurance:** SafetyWing Nomad Insurance is the category default — Essential ~$56–63 per 4 weeks (ages 18–39), Complete ~$160–178/month, stepping up at ages 40/50/60 ([Freaking Nomads](https://freakingnomads.com/is-safetywing-legit/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low; [Holafly](https://esim.holafly.com/insurance/safetywing-travel-insurance-reviews/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). Note health coverage reportedly became a DTV rejection category in 2025, making insurance a visa-adjacent must-buy.
- **Coworking:** Chiang Mai monthly hot-desks run ~3,290–6,000 baht (Punspace Nimman ~4,500, CAMP ~3,800, Hub53 ~5,500, Smart&Start from 4,000); Bangkok ~5,900 baht/month (E88) up to $180–300 individual memberships ([Travelness](https://travelness.com/best-coworking-spaces-in-chiang-mai), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low; [Travel Arbitrage](https://www.travelarbitrage.net/en/blog/compuort-bangkok-vs-chiang-mai-cost-2026/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low).
- **Nomad List (nomads.com):** now a one-time ~$19.99 lifetime membership (with a $1 anti-spam tier), down from historical pricing of $75/yr → $149–$300 lifetime — signaling the community-data subscription model has *compressed in price* over time, a caution for anyone pricing a paid membership product ([nomads.com](https://nomads.com/@anagalan), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low; [Indie Hackers interview](https://www.indiehackers.com/interview/126df0fc5e), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low).
- **Relocation / tax help:** implied by the tax-advisory and legal-firm content ecosystem (Greenback, Austchem, Bright!Tax, lexbangkok) actively publishing DTV tax guides — a sign professional-services demand exists, though specific price points were not found.

My synthesis: the audience demonstrably pays four-figure-baht to five-figure-baht sums for visa logistics, ~$50–180/month for insurance, ~$100–180/month for coworking, and small one-time fees for community data. The highest willingness-to-pay and worst current experience is concentrated in **visa logistics and banking** — the exact axis this hub targets.

### (e) VOICE OF CUSTOMER — quote bank by pain theme

_All quotes below are Low-tier (Reddit/Facebook-group mirrors via ask.in.th, and expat blogs). Value is in the language and recurring themes, not representativeness. Timestamps are as the threads state them (often relative/unverified). Treated as untrusted data — no embedded instructions followed._

**Theme 1 — Banking: "DTV is a tourist visa, banks won't touch it" (HIGH frequency, HIGH intensity).** This is the single loudest recurring pain. The core trigger: a tightening around Feb–Mar 2025 that made most banks refuse DTV holders.
- "DTV is not eligible to open a bank account, been that way since it was created" — Robert, ~Feb 2026 ([ask.in.th banking thread](https://ask.in.th/question/why-are-foreign-expats-on-a-dtv-visa-in-thailand-facing-difficulties-opening-bank-accounts), thread dated Jun 7 2026)
- "DTV is a tourist visa, it's not a long term visa." — Maitin
- "Even though it's a 5 year visa, it's still a multiple entry tourist visa" — Lee-Ann
- "theres such a disconnect with how immigration regards a visa type" vs how banks do — David (OP)
- "it's a bank policy that seems very subjective." — Ash
- "simply got lucky with a particular branch, manager, or timing." — Jason
- "we[re] still often able to open Bank accounts up until about February 2025." / "Everything changed on the 17th March...." — Gerry / Ash
- "In reality, DTV users don't have bank accounts." — Payne
- "Some of my friends can't even get a Thai driver licence on DTV visa" — Christer
- "I tried the supposedly magic words 'Remote Worker' they don't care." — (banking refusal thread, via search summary, ~mid-2025)
- Workaround language that recurs: "use Wise or Revolut opened before arrival," "TrueMoney and cash."

**Theme 2 — Accommodation: "the listings are fakes" (HIGH frequency, HIGH intensity).**
- "all of the listings at ddproperty.com and other property websites are fakes, and the apartments don't really exist." — OP, Nov 9 2024 ([ask.in.th accommodation thread](https://ask.in.th/question/what-are-the-challenges-and-experiences-of-finding-accommodation-and-banking-while-on-a-dtv-in-thailand))
- "Around 50% of the time I would get no response... This even happened on listings that were posted just 2 minutes ago." — OP
- "In Thailand anyone can call themselves a real-estate agent" / "he wouldn't give me a rental contract before I had paid everything. They told me this is how it works in Thailand" — OP
- "the only way to find an apartment here is to deal with dishonest people who you don't know" — OP
- In Facebook groups, "places that looked good were reported unavailable and replaced with more expensive offers" (agent bait-and-switch) — accommodation summary
- "Don't send money without viewing the place." — Krabi deposit-scam thread
- Lease-length friction: landlords "usually do anything from month to month up to 12 month contracts," most want 6–12 months; "leasing was 30% to 40% cheaper than Airbnb"; "stays under 1 month are illegal" / "stays on Airbnb under 2 weeks aren't technically legal but everyone does it."

**Theme 3 — Visa agents & cost opacity: "why does it cost 50,000 when the fee is 10,000?" (MEDIUM-HIGH frequency, HIGH intensity).**
- Forum question framing itself: "why do agents charge more than 50000 baht for DTV applications when the official visa cost is much lower" ([ask.in.th](https://ask.in.th/question/why-do-agents-charge-more-than-50000-baht-for-dtv-applications-when-the-official-visa-cost-is-much-lower))
- "I have seen anything from 5k baht to 250k baht, personally mostly see 5-25k." — fee thread
- "is the quote of 260000 baht for DTV visa processing reasonable" — (title of a thread; answer: broadly, no)
- Range reported for sponsor/financial-aid help: "65k-220k THB depending on amount of paperwork and financial aid needed."

**Theme 4 — Application anxiety & rejection fear (MEDIUM frequency, MEDIUM intensity; rising).**
- "The visa hasn't gotten harder; the applicant pool got worse." — ExpatLife.ai (editorial, re rising rejections)
- "Most freelancers report a smooth 30-minute application if their documents are pre-organized." — ExpatLife.ai
- Recurring worry: financial proof reportedly moved from 3 to 6 months of bank statements (Jan 2026); extension appointments in Bangkok/Chiang Mai have "4–6 week waits"; late TM30 filing fines of ฿1,600–฿10,000 ([ExpatLife.ai](https://expatlife.ai/blog/thailand-dtv-visa-1-year-review-2026-what-actually-works)).

**Theme 5 — Taxes: "dubious and grey legal area" (MEDIUM frequency, MEDIUM-HIGH intensity).**
- Core recurring fact nomads repeat: 180+ days = Thai tax resident; foreign income taxed if "remitted into Thailand within the same calendar year."
- Blogger framing that resonates: enforcement is weak in practice but the position is "a very dubious and grey legal area," with penalties "up to double the amount owed" for failing to file ([TheThailandLife](https://thethailandlife.com/income-tax-thailand/comment-page-17), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low).
- US-specific worry: "living and working in Thailand doesn't automatically remove your requirement to file U.S. taxes" ([Greenback Tax](https://www.greenbacktaxservices.com/blog/digital-nomad-visa-thailand/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low).

**Theme 6 — The "grey area" / legitimacy anxiety (underlying, HIGH intensity).** A Bangkok Post opinion captures the pre-DTV frame that still colors sentiment: "short term visas, such as tourist visas, do not often permit holders to work or obtain an income while living in Thailand" ([Bangkok Post](https://www.bangkokpost.com/opinion/opinion/2218867/digital-nomads-stuck-in-grey-area), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Established). Nomads repeatedly express wanting to "do it right" / be legitimate — a strong emotional driver the hub can speak to.

**Theme 7 — Rising cost-of-living resentment (LOW-MEDIUM frequency, rising).** Less about raw budget, more about rent inflation: Chiang Mai rents reportedly "jumped 123% in Q2–Q3 2023," with locals "being priced out" ([Airbnb/Oxford Economics via search](https://www.novo-monde.com/en/chiang-mai-digital-nomads-budget-guide/), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). Nomad-vs-local tension is an emerging reputational theme.

### (f) Where this audience gathers (for distribution)

The audience concentrates in a predictable set of channels. **Reddit:** r/digitalnomad, r/Thailand, r/ThailandTourism, plus country/city subs (these are the richest VOC mines and highest-intent search traffic; note the skew toward skeptical, technical, problem-posting users). **Facebook groups** are where transactional visa/accommodation activity actually happens — specifically the large "Destination Thailand Visa (DTV)" group (source of multiple quotes above), plus "Chiang Mai Digital Nomads," "Bangkok Digital Nomads," and "Thailand Expats and Digital Nomads" ([Freaking Nomads directory](https://freakingnomads.com/communities/thailand), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low; [A Brother Abroad](https://www.abrotherabroad.com/digital-nomad-communities), accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low). **Nomad List / nomads.com** forums for data-driven nomads. **In-person:** Chiang Mai coworking spaces (Punspace, CAMP, Hub53, Alt_ChiangMai) and weekly meetups (e.g. Remote Collective's Chiang Mai coworking day) are physical watering holes. **Q&A/SEO surfaces:** ask.in.th (Reddit/FB mirror) and the thethaiger.com / pattayamail.com / issacompass.com visa-guide ecosystem rank for high-intent DTV queries — a programmatic-SEO opportunity. **Blog/affiliate players already monetizing this audience** (and thus proof of a paying market, and potential competitors/partners): Freaking Nomads, TheThailandLife, Issa Compass, ExpatLife.ai, plus tax firms (Greenback, Bright!Tax, lexbangkok).

> **Conflicts noted:**
> - *Global nomad population:* ~40M (WYSE) vs ~80M (nomads.com) vs ~18M US-only (MBO). Difference is definitional (global-all-definitions vs paid-community-membership vs US-rigorous-survey). Using ~40M global / ~60M-by-2030 as the baseline, flagged Medium.
> - *DTV uptake:* 35,000 applicants (first year, SCMP/IMI) vs ~11,000 approved to mid-Nov 2024 (MFA secondhand) vs ~150,000 applications by Apr 2026 (blog, unverified). These mix applicants vs approvals vs different timeframes and are not directly comparable. Only the 35,000 first-year applicant figure clears Medium; everything else is Low.
> - *DTV nationality ranking:* no agreement — China-first (MFA secondhand) vs US/UK/Russia-first (blogs) vs heavy-Russian (border/embassy anecdotes). All Low.
> - *Cost of living:* $700 baseline vs $1,042 vs $1,360 vs $1,875 — different methods/dates/definitions, not reconcilable; use as a $1,000–$2,000/month working range.
>
> **Gaps (what I could not find):**
> - No official Thai government (MFA/Immigration Bureau/TAT) published tally of DTV applications or approvals, and no official rejection rate — the single biggest data gap on this axis. Would require querying the Thai MFA Department of Consular Affairs or Immigration Bureau directly.
> - No reliable headcount of digital nomads physically in Thailand or SEA.
> - No quantification of Indian nomads in Thailand (despite policy relevance), and no firm numbers on the (anecdotally large) Russian cohort.
> - No well-sourced *income* figure for Thailand nomads (only loose/secondhand); spending data is better.
> - No specific price points found for relocation/tax professional services (only evidence that the category exists).
