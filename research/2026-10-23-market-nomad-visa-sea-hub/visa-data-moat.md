## Visa Data, Regulatory & Content Moat

*Axis: Can a solo builder maintain a trustworthy, current visa dataset for Thailand/SEA, and is doing so legally safe? All rule claims are stated "as of 2026-10-23." Visa rules in this region change frequently; treat every figure as perishable and re-verify against primary sources before republishing.*

### (a) The visa landscape to cover

**Thailand — Destination Thailand Visa (DTV).** The DTV is the flagship remote-worker/"workcation" visa and the single most important page any Thailand nomad hub must own. Per an official Royal Thai Embassy page, it is a special multiple-entry tourist visa valid **5 years**, granting **up to 180 days of stay per entry**, built for three tracks: (1) "Workcation" — digital nomads, freelancers and remote workers earning from abroad; (2) Thai "Soft Power" activities — Muay Thai, Thai cooking, sports training, medical treatment, seminars, festivals; and (3) spouses/dependent children of DTV holders. Holders **cannot** get a Thai work permit, work for Thai companies, or do freelance work for Thai clients. The financial requirement is **evidence of at least 500,000 THB** in assets (6-month bank statement, payslips or sponsorship letter; crypto not accepted). The embassy page lists an application fee of **€350 (multiple entry, non-refundable)** and notes the visa fee "varies by consulate, typically USD 400–500" — note this conflicts with the widely-repeated "10,000 THB" government fee figure, which likely reflects the baht-denominated fee at some missions ([Royal Thai Embassy Budapest — DTV](https://budapest.thaiembassy.org/en/publicservice/destination-thailand-visa-dtv) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Primary); [EY Tax Alert — Thailand new visa exemptions & DTV](https://ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-gl/technical/tax-alerts/documents/ey-thailand-implements-new-visa-exemptions-for-nationals-of-over-90-jurisdictions-and-new-digital-nomad-visa.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established)).

Two DTV facts are genuinely contested and are exactly the kind of high-value, correction-prone content a moat is built on:
- **The in-country 180-day extension fee.** The official embassy page gives *no* extension fee and says to contact the Immigration Bureau in person. Forum/agent reports split between **1,900 THB** (the standard extension-of-stay fee) and **10,000 THB**, with reports that the amount varies by local immigration office (Phuket reportedly quoting 10,000; others 1,900). Confidence: **Low** — no primary source confirms a single figure ([ask.in.th DTV extension Q&A](https://ask.in.th/question/what-is-the-cost-to-renew-the-dtv-visa-after-180-days-in-thailand) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low)). This is a prime "verify at your local office" disclaimer candidate.
- **The 31 August 2026 rule change.** From **31 August 2026**, DTV applications must be filed at the Thai mission covering the applicant's **country of nationality or legal permanent residence** (ending the "Laos run"/third-country filing), and a **criminal-record/police-clearance certificate** is now required (reported as issued within 6 months, in English or certified translation). Sources differ on whether the certificate must come from the country of nationality or the country of application. Confidence: **Medium** (multiple law-firm/consultant sources agree on substance; embassy notices are authoritative) ([Thai Law Online](https://www.thailawonline.com/thai-legal-news/no-more-laos-runs-from-31-august-the-dtv-must-be-filed-in-your-home-country/) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low); [Issa Compass — DTV home-country rule](https://www.issacompass.com/insights/thailand-dtv-apply-country-of-citizenship-or-residence-rule-change) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low)).

**Thailand — Long-Term Resident (LTR) visa.** A **10-year** residence visa run by the Board of Investment (BOI), with categories for Wealthy Global Citizens, Wealthy Pensioners, Work-From-Thailand Professionals, and Highly-Skilled Professionals, plus dependents; fee commonly reported at **50,000 THB/person** for the full term. On **4 February 2025** the BOI issued Announcement No. Por. 3/2568 relaxing criteria — notably **removing the USD 80,000/yr personal-income requirement for Wealthy Global Citizens** and easing income/experience rules and the dependent definition (some dependent changes pending a Ministry of Interior announcement) ([Tilleke & Gibbins — Thailand eases LTR requirements](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/thailand-eases-requirements-for-long-term-resident-visas/) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established); [KPMG Thailand Tax News Flash #151](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/th/pdf/2025/02/th-tax-news-flash-issue-151.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established)). *Analysis: exact per-category thresholds should be pulled from the BOI's own ltr.boi.go.th criteria, which this search did not fetch directly — flag as a data-sourcing to-do.*

**Thailand — tourist / visa-exempt rules (the biggest recent churn).** Thailand **loosened** short stays in mid-2024 (Cabinet approved 28 May 2024; ~93 countries got 60-day visa-exempt entry, up from 30, alongside the DTV launch) and then **reversed course in 2026**. Per Royal Gazette regulations dated **31 August 2026**, effective **15 September 2026** (15 days after publication), the visa-exempt stay for most of those nationalities reverts to **30 days** (reported capped at two entries/year), with up to a 30-day extension at officer discretion (60 days total). Standard tourist-visa (TR) holders get 60 days + a 30-day extension (90 total). Existing stamps remain valid until expiry. Confidence: **High** on the loosen-then-tighten pattern; **Medium** on exact current caps (Gazette text not fetched directly) ([Freaking Nomads — Thailand cuts visa-free to 30 days](https://freakingnomads.com/news/thailand-officially-cuts-visa-free-stay-to-30-days/) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low); [EY Tax Alert (2024 expansion)](https://ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-gl/technical/tax-alerts/documents/ey-thailand-implements-new-visa-exemptions-for-nationals-of-over-90-jurisdictions-and-new-digital-nomad-visa.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established)).

**Thailand — Privilege (ex-"Elite") visa.** Thailand Privilege Card Co., Ltd. sells five paid membership tiers (figures consistent across broker sources, VAT included): **Bronze 650,000 THB / 5 yr; Gold 900,000 THB / 5 yr; Platinum 1,500,000 THB / 10 yr; Diamond 2,500,000 THB / 15 yr; Reserve 5,000,000 THB / 20 yr (invitation only)**. No right to work. Conflicts exist on Bronze's closing date (31 Dec 2026 vs withdrawn earlier) and per-dependent fees — unsettled ([The Thaiger — Thailand Privilege tiers](https://thethaiger.com/visas/thailand-privilege/) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low)).

**SEA peers (see table).** Indonesia's **E33G Remote Worker KITAS** (Bali's "digital nomad visa," live since April 2024), Malaysia's **DE Rantau Nomad Pass**, **Vietnam** (no dedicated nomad visa — 90-day e-visa plus a *proposed* Golden Visa), and the **Philippines** (Digital Nomad Visa authorized by Executive Order No. 86, signed 24 April 2025, but reportedly not yet live on the DFA e-Visa portal as of 2026) round out the regional coverage a hub needs.

### (b) How often rules change, and the primary sources to track

The 2024–2026 record shows **Thailand alone made at least two major short-stay reversals in ~24 months** (60-day expansion in mid-2024, 30-day rollback effective 15 Sept 2026), launched the DTV (mid-2024), materially revised DTV filing rules (31 Aug 2026), and relaxed LTR criteria (Feb 2025) — plus a live tax-residency change (2024) and proposed tax relief (2025). This pace is the core argument *for* a maintained hub and *against* static content. Confidence: **High** (multiple Established + Primary sources corroborate the timeline).

**Authoritative/primary sources to track (recommended monitoring list):**
- Thailand: Royal Thai Government e-Visa portal (**thaievisa.go.th**); Thai Immigration Bureau (**immigration.go.th**); Ministry of Foreign Affairs & individual Royal Thai Embassy "public service" visa pages (each mission publishes its own checklist — they diverge); BOI LTR site (**ltr.boi.go.th**); the **Royal Gazette (ratchakitcha.soc.go.th)** for the legally-operative text; Revenue Department for tax.
- Indonesia: Directorate General of Immigration (**imigrasi.go.id** / evisa.imigrasi.go.id).
- Malaysia: **MDEC De Rantau** portal (FAQ currently v6.0, Mar 2025) and Immigration Dept.
- Vietnam: official e-visa portal (evisa.xuatnhapcanh.gov.vn).
- Philippines: DFA **e-Visa portal (evisa.gov.ph)**.
*Analysis: embassy pages are authoritative but fragmented and per-mission; the Royal Gazette is the only place a rule is legally final, so the hub's editorial process should treat press/agent coverage as a lead and the Gazette/embassy notice as the citation.*

### (c) Feasibility of a solo-maintained "visa rules by nationality × country" dataset

A **general** passport-visa matrix is cheaply obtainable; a **current, nomad-specific** one is not. The open **ilyankou/passport-index-dataset** (GitHub, **MIT license**) covers **199 countries** in CSV (matrix + tidy forms, ISO-2/ISO-3/name variants) with requirement codes (`visa free`, `visa on arrival`, `eta`, `e-visa`, `visa required`, `no admission`, numeric visa-free day counts). **Critically it is archived — last updated 12 Jan 2025** — and points newer data to **github.com/imorte/passport-index-data (Feb 2026 onward)** ([ilyankou/passport-index-dataset](https://github.com/ilyankou/passport-index-dataset) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Primary [repo itself])). The official **Passport Index API is request-only** (government agencies prioritized), not open ([Passport Index API request](https://discover.passportindex.org/?p=10247) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established)). The authoritative commercial source, **IATA Timatic** (now delivered via Amadeus; the consumer "IATA Travel Centre"/TIM app was decommissioned 31 Mar 2025), draws on **1,800+ government sources across 220 countries** but publishes **no public price** — access requires contacting IATA/Amadeus sales, and historic licensing disputes (IATA v. ICTS, settled 2016) show the data is actively protected IP ([Amadeus — Timatic](https://amadeus.com/de/fluggesellschaften/produkte/timatic) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established); [IATA/ICTS settlement](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/pressroom-archive/2016-releases/2016-12-19-01/) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary)).

*Analysis (not sourced fact): A solo builder can seed a passport × destination tourist-entry matrix from the MIT dataset legally, but (1) it is stale and must be refreshed, and (2) it answers "can I enter as a tourist," **not** the nomad-relevant questions — DTV eligibility, income thresholds, work-permit prohibitions, extension mechanics — which no open dataset covers. Those are hand-curated per country and are precisely where the maintenance labor and the moat live. Reselling Timatic/Henley/PassportIndex data is licensing-gated; scraping their outputs risks ToS/IP claims. The defensible build is: open matrix as a base layer + a hand-maintained, cited "nomad rules" layer for ~6–8 SEA countries, which is realistically solo-maintainable only if scoped narrowly and backed by a source-monitoring routine.*

### (d) Liability / legal risk — publishing info vs. offering "visa help"

Two distinct risk layers. **Publishing visa information** is low-risk and standard, *if* framed as information, not advice. Every established visa site uses a three-part disclaimer: (1) "**does not constitute legal/immigration advice** or a professional-client relationship"; (2) rules are "**subject to change without notice**" and no guarantee of currency/accuracy; (3) "**verify with official government sources / consult a licensed immigration lawyer, registered adviser, or the relevant embassy**," plus a blanket liability disclaimer for actions taken on the content ([composite of myvisa.com.au](https://myvisa.com.au/disclaimer), [visaenvoy.com](https://visaenvoy.com/disclaimer/), [one-visa.com disclaimers](https://www.one-visa.com/one-visa-disclaimer/) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low)). *Analysis: an "as-of date + primary-source link on every rule" editorial policy is both the disclaimer and the moat.*

**Offering a paid "visa help" service is where regulation bites, and it is jurisdiction-specific.** Precedent from regulated markets: in the **UK**, giving immigration advice/services in the course of business without authorization is a **criminal offence** (Immigration & Asylum Act 1999, s.91; regulator renamed from OISC to the **Immigration Advice Authority** in Jan 2025), and prosecutions are active — the rule catches even not-for-profit help; only a personal favor from a friend/family is exempt ([Immigration Advice Authority (Wikipedia → statute)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_Advice_Authority) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low, used to locate statute); [ILPA — Regulation of immigration advice](https://ilpa.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/resources/33352/17.06.27-Regulation-of-immigration-advice-and-services.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established)). The US analog is "**notario fraud**"/unauthorized practice of law enforcement (not directly sourced here — gap).

**In Thailand specifically:** full legal practice (court representation, binding Thai-law opinions, a lawyer's licence) is **reserved to Thai nationals** under the Lawyers Act B.E. 2528 (1985), s.35; a foreigner cannot hold a full licence ([APEC Legal Services Inventory — Thailand](https://legalservices.apec.org/inventory/thailand.html) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: High, tier: Established)). **However**, a Tilleke overview notes there is **no legal restriction on lay persons giving legal advice outside the court system** — a licence is mandatory only for trial litigators ([Tilleke — Overview of the Legal Profession in Thailand](https://www.tilleke.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/NEW2__CH01-An-Overview-of-the-Legal-Profession-in-Thailand_20190515_0.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established)). Separately, Thailand runs a **certified-agent scheme for LTR/long-stay applications**: to be an accredited agent a private entity must be a juristic person with **≥5,000,000 THB paid-up capital** (or a registered Thai association), publish fee schedules/procedures, and begin screening clients within 90 days — i.e., formal visa brokerage for the BOI track is a licensed, capital-gated activity ([Thailand.go.th — Conditions for certified LTR agents](https://www.thailand.go.th/event-detail/006_067) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary)). And any foreigner-owned service business in Thailand generally needs a Foreign Business Licence/Certificate under the **Foreign Business Act List 3** unless exempted ([Tilleke — FBA/new exemptions](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/understanding-new-work-permit-law/2/) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Established)).

*Analysis (not sourced fact): The safe lane for a solo builder is clearly a **content/information + lead-gen** business (publish cited rules; refer paid cases to licensed Thai lawyers/accredited agents and earn referral/affiliate revenue), not a "we'll file your visa" service. A hands-on filing service done from inside Thailand by a foreigner implicates (a) FBA licensing, (b) possibly the certified-agent regime for BOI visas, and (c) work-permit/UPL exposure if clients are from regulated markets like the UK. Disclaimers reduce publishing liability but do **not** cure providing an unauthorized regulated service.*

### (e) Why visa content resists AI commoditization (the moat)

*This subsection is analysis built on the sourced facts above, not a sourced claim itself.* The moat is **recency + liability + primary-source traceability**, the three things a generic LLM answer is worst at. (1) **Recency:** the 2024–2026 record shows rules flipping within months (60→30-day reversal; 31 Aug 2026 DTV filing change); an LLM's training cut-off makes it structurally prone to citing the *old* rule confidently — exactly the failure mode users have been burned by. (2) **Liability/stakes:** a wrong visa answer costs a denied entry, a fine, or deportation (Indonesia reportedly deported 62 foreigners in a single month for working on tourist visas), so users are strongly motivated to click through to an **authoritative, current, dated source** rather than trust an unattributed paragraph. (3) **Traceability:** the defensible asset is each rule carrying an "as-of date + Royal Gazette/embassy link," which an AI answer does not provide and which is also the disclaimer that limits legal risk. (4) **Fragmentation of truth:** rules live across per-mission embassy checklists, the Gazette, BOI, and provincial immigration offices that interpret the same rule differently (the DTV extension fee being the textbook case) — synthesizing and *continuously re-checking* that is manual, local-knowledge work. The flip side: this moat is a **treadmill**, not a fortress — it exists only while the dataset is actually kept current, which ties the business's defensibility directly to the maintenance discipline discussed in (c).

**Adjacent content/tool opportunities surfaced (all high-churn, high-anxiety → high engagement):**
- **Thai tax residency / 180-day rule + foreign-income remittance.** Thailand now taxes foreign income *remitted* to Thailand regardless of year earned (for income earned from 1 Jan 2024), but only for **tax residents = 180+ days in a calendar year**; a 2025 proposal to relax this (same-year or following-year remittance exemption) was reported but unconfirmed as enacted — a natural "DTV + tax" calculator/explainer ([terms.law — Thai remittance tax 2024](https://terms.law/Thai/tax/remittance-tax-2024.html) / [180-day residency guide](https://terms.law/Thai/tax/tax-residency-180-days.html) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Medium, tier: Low)).
- **90-day reporting, TM30 address notification, re-entry permits.** 90-day reports filable online ~15 days before/7 after due date; TM30 filed by landlord/owner and often a precondition for 90-day reports and extensions; fines reported ~2,000 THB (90-day) and 800–2,000 THB (TM30). Mostly forum/law-firm sourced; **Low** confidence on exact fees/windows — a reminder tool is an obvious retention hook ([Pattaya Mail — TM30 updates](https://www.pattayamail.com/latestnews/news/457902-457902) (accessed 2026-10-23, confidence: Low, tier: Low)).
